## October 24

# Felix of Thibiuca

*North African bishop, refused to surrender the books, d. 303*

The first edict of Diocletian, posted at Nicomedia in February of 303, reached Roman North Africa in the early summer. It commanded the Christian clergy to surrender their *libri sacri* — the codices of the gospels and the epistles, the working bishopric's most valuable physical possession — for public burning. Those who complied became known, in the bitter Latin that followed, as the *traditores,* the *handers-over.* Those who refused became the proto-martyrs of the persecution. Felix, bishop of the small *municipium* of Thibiuca in Africa Proconsularis — about thirty-five Roman miles southwest of Carthage — was the first North African bishop publicly to refuse.

The trial survives in detail. Felix was sixty-six years old, bishop for some years; the local *curator,* Magnelianus, was an old neighbor, and the conversation between them in the city forum was constrained by familiarity as much as by procedure. *I have the books,* Felix said. *Bring them out and burn them,* Magnelianus answered. *I will not.* *Then surrender them to me.* *I will not.* *Why?* *Because it is better that I should be burned than they.*

He was sent in chains to Carthage, then held a fortnight while the proconsul Anulinus tried persuasion, then put on a ship to Italy when the proconsul declined to execute him in his own province. The execution was carried out at Venusia in southern Apulia — the small inland town where Horace had been born three centuries earlier — by the local magistrate on instructions from Rome. Felix was beheaded on the thirtieth of August, 303. The day his community at Thibiuca received the news from Italy is what the African calendar afterwards observed.

Five of his deacons and lectors died with him: Audactus, Januarius, Fortunatianus, Septiminus, and a sixth whose name varies in the manuscripts. They too had refused. *Better that I should be burned than they* became one of the small permanent sayings of the African church. Within a generation the *traditores* who had handed over their codices for the fire would become the central case of the Donatist controversy that tore the African church apart — and the men of Thibiuca who had not handed them over would be the heroes both sides could agree on.

> *"Better that I should be burned than they."* — Felix, in the forum at Thibiuca
