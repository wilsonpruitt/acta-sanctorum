## February 12

# Benedict of Aniane

*Carolingian monastic reformer, c. 750–821*

He was born Witiza, a Visigothic noble in Languedoc, raised at the Frankish court as a hostage and serving Pepin the Short and then Charlemagne as a royal cup-bearer. He was a soldier — at Charlemagne's Italian campaign of 773 — when he saw his elder brother go under in the river Tessin during a fording, and he leapt in to save him. He nearly drowned himself. The chronicle says that as the water closed over his head, he made the vow that if God brought him out, he would leave the king's service. God brought him out. He left.

He went first to the abbey of Saint-Seine near Dijon, where he took the name Benedict — out of admiration for the founder, whose Rule he had begun to study seriously — and stayed several years as a novice and then choir monk. He found Saint-Seine too lax. He returned to his own estates at Aniane in the Languedoc and founded a stricter house there. Other reformers heard of him. By 814 he was effectively the leading monastic figure in the empire, and Charlemagne's son Louis the Pious — who had grown up admiring him — made him general overseer of all imperial monasteries.

At the Synod of Aachen in 817 he led the Carolingian Church to a single binding standard of Benedictine observance for all royal foundations. *One Rule and one custom*, he insisted. The empire's monasteries, which had drifted into local variants for two centuries, were brought back into a common pattern. He built one more monastery, Inden, near Aachen, as the model house for the empire. He died there in 821.

What he gave the Latin Church was uniformity at a moment when it could have lost its bearings, and a tradition that would feed Cluny, the Cistercians, and every reform of Benedictine life that followed.

> *"One Rule and one custom."* — Benedict of Aniane, at the Synod of Aachen
