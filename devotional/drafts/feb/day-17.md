## February 17

# Finan of Lindisfarne

*Bishop, d. 661*

He was an Irish monk of Iona, trained in the Columban tradition, when Aidan died at Lindisfarne in 651 and the brothers there asked Iona to send a successor. Iona sent Finan. He inherited a diocese that covered most of Northumbria — twice the size of the kingdom Aidan had begun with — and a missionary task that was very much unfinished.

He built. Aidan's wooden church on the tidal island of Lindisfarne had grown too small. Finan put up a new one of split oak roofed with thatch, large enough for the brothers and the visiting kings and the catechumens together. He continued Aidan's habit of constant travel through the kingdom, on foot, preaching at the country crossroads where Christianity was still only a court religion. Bede records two of his great baptisms: Peada, the Mercian prince, who married Alhflaed of Northumbria on the condition that he and his nobles receive baptism; and Sigeberht, the king of the East Saxons, who came north to be instructed and went home converted with four priests Finan had given him. From those two baptisms came the Christianization of central and southeastern England.

He did not enjoy controversy and could not always avoid it. The Roman observance of Easter was advancing in southern England; Wilfrid, just back from Rome, was arguing for it; Iona held to the Celtic computation. Finan defended the Celtic side without rancor and died before the Synod of Whitby could force the question. His successor Colman would lose at Whitby; Finan, mercifully, did not have to.

He died in 661 after ten years at Lindisfarne. Bede, no friend of the Celtic computation, nevertheless writes of Finan with respect bordering on affection. *He was a man*, Bede says, *zealous in good works, of an apostolic conversation.*
