## September 29

# Michael, and All the Holy Angels

*Archangel and the heavenly host, feast known as Michaelmas*

The day was originally a dedication. Around the year 490 a herdsman named Gargano, looking for a stray bull on the rocky promontory that bears his name in southern Italy, found the animal kneeling at the mouth of a cave high on Monte Sant'Angelo. He shot an arrow at it; the arrow turned in the air and struck the herdsman. The local bishop, hearing the story, fasted and prayed; an angel appeared and identified himself as Michael, *guardian of this place,* and asked for a church on the cave. The shrine raised over the cave's entrance — the basilica of San Michele Arcangelo on Monte Gargano — became, with Saint James at Compostela and the tomb of the apostles in Rome, one of the three great pilgrimage sites of medieval Western Christianity. Its dedication day, the twenty-ninth of September, the Latin church kept thereafter as the feast of Michael and all the angels.

The Bollandists trace the spread of the cult: the second basilica that Pope Boniface raised at Rome on the Circus Maximus in the seventh century; the third on the Via Salaria seven miles from the city; the great Norman foundation of Mont-Saint-Michel rising out of the tide on the border of Normandy and Brittany after a vision in 708; the further chain of Michael shrines that ran the whole length of the *via Sacra Langobardorum* down the Italian peninsula and across the Channel to Cornwall and Skellig Michael off the Irish coast. The angel of the Apocalypse who fights the dragon, of Daniel who guards the people of Israel, of Jude's letter who would not bring railing accusation against the devil but said only *the Lord rebuke thee* — Michael is the angel the church set as the patron of soldiers, of the dying, of high places, of the moment when one age yields to another.

The day in the West has always belonged to the angels generally. The Eastern church keeps the *Synaxis of the Bodiless Powers* on November 8 — Michael, Gabriel, Raphael, Uriel, the cherubim, the seraphim, the thrones, the dominions — but the Latin tradition has kept the autumn feast as a feast of the whole heavenly host. The crops are in by the twenty-ninth in northern Europe; the rents are due; the equinox has just turned. The angels who watch the changing of the seasons are remembered when the season changes.

> *"Quis ut Deus?"* — *Michael's name in Hebrew: Who is like God?*
