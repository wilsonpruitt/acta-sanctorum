## June 13

# Anthony of Padua

*Franciscan preacher and doctor, 1195–1231*

He was born Fernando at Lisbon, son of a knight of King Sancho I of Portugal. He entered the canons regular of Saint Augustine at fifteen and was sent at nineteen to the abbey of Santa Cruz at Coimbra, where he spent nine years reading. He could have been a doctor of the schools. He never wrote a treatise. The moment of his conversion came in 1220, when the bodies of five Franciscans, killed by Moors in Morocco, were brought back to Coimbra in their habits. He looked at them, went home, and asked that day for the Franciscan habit himself. He took the name Anthony, after the church of San Antonio where the friars dwelt, and set sail to follow the five.

He never reached it. A fever drove him back; storms blew the ship to Sicily; he made his way to Assisi in May 1221 for the great chapter and was assigned, as a man of no apparent use, to the kitchen of the hermitage of Monte Paolo above Forlì. He swept and washed pots for a year. The accident of an unprepared preacher at an ordination Mass in 1222 produced the discovery: he was made to preach extempore and was found to be the most learned scriptural preacher in Italy. Francis gave him reluctant permission to teach theology to the brothers — the first such permission within the order — and sent him out.

He spent the last nine years of his life preaching. He preached in Lombardy, in Provence, in the Romagna, sometimes to thirty thousand in the open piazzas of Padua and Rimini, who came barefoot from the surrounding hills. He preached on the prophets, on the parables, on usury, on reconciliation, on what conscience could be argued back from. At Rimini, where many heretics would not be moved, he won them back to the faith — among them a heresiarch named Bonovillus, thirty years blinded by error, whom he led to the light of truth and who held to the Church's teaching to the end.

He died of edema at thirty-six, near Padua, on June 13, 1231, his last words a vision of Christ in the hands of a friar holding a candle. He was canonized inside the year — the fastest in church history.
