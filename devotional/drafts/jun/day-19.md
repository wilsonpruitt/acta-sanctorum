## June 19

# Bruno of Querfurt

*Camaldolese missionary-bishop, protomartyr of the order, c. 974–1008*

The AASS Junii III calls him *Bonifacius* — the name he took in religion in honor of the apostle of Germany, and the name he carried through the brief, intense, eight years of his apostolic mission to the pagan north. He had been born about 974 at Querfurt in Saxony of a noble family. He came to Romuald's Camaldolese hermitage at Pereum near Ravenna, exchanged silk and a train of attendants for a single cloak, and so outstripped his fellow hermits in austerity that his life was held a wonder. From there he asked the Pope's leave to carry the Gospel north, where Adalbert of Prague had been martyred in 997.

He went, under the commission of Pope Gregory V, to the pagan country east of Prussia. He baptized in rivers, taught through interpreters, lived rough. The natives, the chronicler notes, feared that if they killed him as they had killed Adalbert, the miracles afterward would convert their people; so by a cunning malice they held back their hands a long time, unwilling to give him the death he desired.

The chronicler keeps one miracle. The pagan king of the region, refusing baptism, had a great pyre built before his hall and challenged Bruno to walk through it as proof of his faith. He walked through it unharmed, *per ignem transisset illæsus*. The king and his people were baptized, and the king resolved never to leave Bruno's side. But the king had a brother who would not believe, and the king killed that unbelieving brother himself. A second brother, who lived apart, was enraged at the conversion; when Bruno came to him he would not hear his word, and — fearing the king would take the saint from his hands — had him beheaded on the spot. At once that brother and all who stood by were struck blind and rigid as stones. The king came, prayed for them with the other Christians, and their sense was restored; they sought penance, received baptism, and raised a church over the martyr's body. The chronicler dates the death to 1008. The AASS keeps his feast on June 19.

He had wanted, the *Vita* by Peter Damian says, to spill blood for Christ from the first day he had taken the Camaldolese habit. The natives had refused him a long time. They had refused him their conversion as long as they could. They gave him both in the same hour.

> *"He passed through the fire unharmed, baptized the king and the people, and was killed by the king's raging brother."* — Baronius's *Roman Martyrology*, quoted at AASS Junii III, on Bruno
