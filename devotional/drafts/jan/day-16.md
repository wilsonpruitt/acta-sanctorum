## January 16

# Marcellus I, Pope

*Bishop of Rome, c. 255–309*

He was elected to the see of Rome in the year 308, five years after the end of the Diocletian persecution. The Roman Church he inherited was not so much divided as winded. Christians who had handed over the Scriptures under torture wished to be received back; Christians who had not been broken did not wish to receive them. The previous pope, Marcellinus, had himself faltered under interrogation and died under a cloud; the see had been vacant for several years. Marcellus took up the office knowing he would be hated by half the city before his first year was out.

He set up a disciplinary system for the *lapsi* — the lapsed. The penitents would do public penance for a fixed period, take their place at the back of the assembly, and only after years be readmitted to communion. The unbroken Christians thought it too lenient; the lapsed thought it cruel. Both factions rioted. Marcellus, in the same months, was also organizing Rome's first twenty-five *tituli*, the parish churches that would still be the bones of the city's Christianity in our day.

When the riots threatened public order, the emperor Maxentius — no friend of Christianity, but a friend of order — exiled Marcellus from the city. Tradition says he was forced to work the imperial stables on the road south, and died there. Damasus, half a century later, would write his epitaph: *true shepherd, because he proclaimed to the lapsed that they must weep for their crimes, he was reckoned a bitter enemy by all the unhappy ones.*

His was a short pontificate. What he gave the Church was not a great gift but a difficult one: the conviction that mercy can be ordered, and that ordering it costs.
