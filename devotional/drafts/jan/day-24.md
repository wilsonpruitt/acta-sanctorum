## January 24

# Babylas of Antioch

*Bishop and martyr, d. 253*

He was bishop of Antioch in the years when the empire briefly had a Christian — or near-Christian — emperor. Philip the Arab, who reigned from 244 to 249, was at minimum sympathetic; some early sources call him outright Christian. In either case Philip arrived at the door of Babylas's church one Easter night, with his retinue, expecting to enter for the vigil.

Babylas met him at the threshold and would not let him in. The emperor, it was widely known, had had his predecessor Gordian III killed to take the throne. Babylas explained, courteously, that an unconfessed killer could not be admitted to the Eucharist. The emperor — to his everlasting credit — submitted: he took his place among the public penitents at the back, did the penance Babylas imposed, and was readmitted in due course. It is the first recorded instance in Christian history of a bishop refusing communion to an emperor. Ambrose would do the same to Theodosius a hundred and forty years later, and would cite Babylas.

Babylas's own end came under Decius, five or six years after the encounter with Philip. He was arrested, refused to sacrifice, and was either killed or died in chains; the chronicles disagree. What no one disagreed about was where to put him: his tomb was placed in the suburb of Daphne, near the famous pagan oracle of Apollo, and the people of Antioch began going to the bishop's bones instead of the god's voice. When the emperor Julian came to consult the oracle a century later, Apollo would not speak. Julian had the bones removed. Apollo still would not speak.

> *"You shall not enter."* — Babylas to Philip the Arab, Easter night
