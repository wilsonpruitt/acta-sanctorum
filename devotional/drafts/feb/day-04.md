## February 4

# Nicholas of Studios

*Abbot, iconodule confessor, c. 793–868*

He was a Cretan boy sent to Constantinople for schooling at ten, placed at the great urban monastery of Studios under his uncle Theophanes. He took the habit in his teens. Studios, by his middle age, was the largest monastery in the Byzantine capital and the central holdout of the orthodox party in the second iconoclast crisis. The emperors Leo V, Michael II, and Theophilus had each, in succession, restored the imperial ban on holy images. The Studite monks had each, in succession, refused.

Nicholas stood with his community. When Theodore the Studite, the great abbot of the preceding generation, was exiled for refusing the ban, Nicholas went with him and shared the exile. When Theodore returned, Nicholas returned. When Theophilus revived the persecution in his own reign, Nicholas was beaten in public, dragged through the streets, imprisoned. The chroniclers describe him as a quiet man who would not argue with his interrogators; he simply said, each time, that he could not stop venerating the image of Christ because Christ had a face.

He outlived three iconoclast emperors. In 843, under the empress Theodora, the icons were restored definitively and the Feast of Orthodoxy was instituted. Nicholas was eventually made abbot of Studios — twice, in fact, with an interval of resignation between — and governed his community to his death at seventy-five. The monastery he handed on was the bones-deep guardian of Byzantine orthodoxy; it survived intact for another four centuries.

What he had refused, all his life, was the imperial offer of an easier image: an offer to settle for the word *without* the icon, the gospel *without* the face. He could not. The Word, he kept saying, had become flesh and dwelt among us, and the flesh had had a face.

> *"I cannot stop venerating the image, because Christ had a face."* — Nicholas, before his interrogators
