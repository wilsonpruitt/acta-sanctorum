## May 25

# Gregory VII, Pope

*Reformer of the Latin Church, c. 1015–1085*

He was born Hildebrand, of small Tuscan parents, sent as a boy to an uncle who was abbot at Santa Maria on the Aventine, and educated thence at the Lateran. He served five popes before his own election — as chaplain to Gregory VI in exile in Germany, as administrator of the patrimony under Leo IX, as legate to France and cardinal archdeacon under Nicholas II, and as chancellor of the Roman Church under Alexander II. He was small, dark, intense, an indifferent Latinist but a relentless administrator. He had spent thirty years preparing the reform he believed the church needed when, at the funeral of Alexander II in April 1073, the Roman crowd carried him off and acclaimed him pope by acclamation in San Pietro in Vincoli before he could refuse.

He took the name of the great Gregory, and he laid down, in the *Dictatus Papae* of 1075, twenty-seven theses on the freedom of the apostolic see. The substance of his pontificate was the substance of those theses: that bishops should not be appointed by kings, that priests should not be married, that simony should be uprooted at every level, that the pope was the spiritual head of all baptized rulers. The conflict that followed — the Investiture Controversy — broke open the high middle ages. Henry IV of Germany, having received the bishops of Worms' deposition decree against him, met him at Canossa in January 1077, stood barefoot in the snow for three days, and was absolved. Three years later the king repudiated the absolution, marched on Rome, and installed an antipope.

Gregory was rescued from Castel Sant'Angelo in 1084 by the Normans of Robert Guiscard, who sacked Rome so brutally in the rescue that the Romans turned against the pope. He withdrew with the Normans to Salerno. He died there on May 25, 1085. The last words the chronicler preserves are these — quoted in every monastery of the reform: *Dilexi iustitiam et odivi iniquitatem, propterea morior in exilio.* I loved justice and hated iniquity; therefore I die in exile.
