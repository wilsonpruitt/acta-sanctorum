## April 22

# Theodore of Sykeon

*Bishop, abbot, c. 540–613*

He was born in a Galatian inn — his mother was a prostitute, his father a Byzantine civil servant passing through — and he was raised, against the odds of his beginnings, into a learned and saintly man. His mother left the trade after his birth and ran the inn with two other women who had also come out of similar lives; the household was poor, hard, and entirely dedicated to scrubbing the boy clean of his inheritance. He had a holy monk for a tutor in his teens. He took the habit at himself perhaps eighteen.

He founded a monastery near the village of Sykeon, in central Anatolia, and governed it for years. His Life — written by his disciple George — is one of the great surviving Byzantine hagiographies: it preserves a daily texture of the Anatolian countryside that no other source gives us. The villagers brought him their possessed, their crops blighted by hail, their sick children, their family quarrels, their unfaithful wives, their drunkard husbands. He treated each case at the length it required. He could exorcize, the chronicler insists, with a touch; he could also spend three weeks counseling a young couple who had simply learned that they did not know each other.

He was made bishop of the local see of Anastasiopolis in 591, against his preference. He governed it for a decade and resigned, exhausted, to return to the monastery he had founded. He served as adviser to the emperor Maurice, then to Phocas who killed Maurice, then to Heraclius who killed Phocas. He buried friends from all three administrations and never lost his attachment to the village of his beginning.

He died at Sykeon in 613, in the cell he had built with his own hands fifty years earlier.

> *"The Lord brought me out of the mud; let me not return to dust before he calls me."* — Theodore, in his last illness
