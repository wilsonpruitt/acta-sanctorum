## February 23

# Polycarp of Smyrna

*Bishop and martyr, c. 69–155*

He was the bridge between the apostles and the Fathers. He had been a disciple, in his youth, of John — the same John who had stood at the cross and survived to old age on Patmos. He was made bishop of Smyrna around 90 and held the see for sixty-five years, longer than most bishops have lived. Irenaeus, as a boy in Smyrna, sat at his feet; Irenaeus would later carry Polycarp's testimony into Gaul and into the second century's defense of orthodoxy. *I remember the very place*, Irenaeus would write, *where the blessed Polycarp used to sit and speak.*

He was eighty-six when the persecution under Antoninus Pius came to Smyrna. The proconsul arrested him on a country estate and brought him into the stadium of the city. The crowd shouted for his blood. The proconsul, who would have preferred not to execute an old man, asked him to curse Christ and go home. Polycarp's answer is preserved in the *Martyrdom of Polycarp*, written by the church at Smyrna within months of the event: *Eighty-six years have I served him, and he has done me no wrong; how then can I blaspheme my king, who has saved me?*

The proconsul ordered him burned. They built the pyre. They tied him to the stake — Polycarp asked that he not be nailed, saying the same God who had given him strength to come up the steps would give him strength to stand in the fire. The fire was lit. The flames, the eyewitness writes, made an arch around him and did not touch him. The executioner had to run him through with a sword. The Christians who collected what remained of his bones for burial wrote down everything they had seen.

The Letter is the oldest non-biblical Christian martyrdom we have. It exists because they could not bear to lose what he had said.
