## June 30

# Erentrude of Salzburg

*Abbess, first of Nonnberg, d. c. 630*

She came to Bavaria because a kinsman asked her to. Rupert of Worms — bishop, missionary, the man the Bavarians would remember as their apostle — had gone east to plant the faith among a half-pagan people and to raise a church on the old Roman ruins of Juvavum, the place now called Salzburg. He wanted a house of women to root the work, and he sent home for Erentrude, his kinswoman, a virgin consecrated to God, the chronicler says, from her first cradle. She left Worms and the Rhine and everything she knew, and came.

He built her dwelling on the height above the river — the Nuns'-Mount, *Nonnberg*, the convent that still keeps its rock over the city after thirteen centuries — and set her over it as its first abbess. She governed, the Life says, so that she counted the salvation of every sister as her own. She washed the feet of the poor with her own hands. She blessed the food and saw it carried to everyone who came to the gate. She had crossed half of Europe to do, in a strange country, the small and daily and unglamorous work of keeping a household for God.

When Rupert died, she would not stop praying for him. Night and day she kept it up, until he came to her himself, in a vision, and gave her the one summons she had left to answer: *Come, sister most dear, into the kingdom of Christ, for which you have now laboured so long.* She fell sick within days and died on the eve of July, and they buried her on her mountain, where the nuns have prayed over her ever since.

> *"I know, Father, I know: for the Lord Jesus Christ revealed it to me in spirit, saying, Go in peace whither you are called."* — Erentrude, to Rupert
