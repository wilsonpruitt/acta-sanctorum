## April 25

# Mark the Evangelist

*Evangelist, first century*

He was John Mark, son of a Mary whose house in Jerusalem was the upper room where the first Christians met. He was the cousin of Barnabas. He went with Paul and Barnabas on the first missionary journey to Cyprus and Asia Minor, lost his nerve at Perga in Pamphylia, and went home. Paul, hard, would not take him on the second journey; Barnabas took him to Cyprus anyway. The split between the two men is one of the small bruises in the Acts of the Apostles. By the time Paul wrote his last letter from a Roman prison years later, the bruise had healed: *Take Mark and bring him with you, for he is useful to me in ministry.*

The early Church remembered him as the disciple of Peter, who had preached the gospel through him. Papias of Hierapolis, writing in the 130s, says Mark wrote down Peter's preaching at the Roman Christians' request. The Gospel that bears his name is the shortest, the briskest, the most physical — the one that pays attention to bodies and weather and the faces of bystanders, and that ends in the original manuscripts with the women fleeing the tomb in fear and silence. It is the Gospel of a man who had stood at the edge of the apostolic generation and watched.

Tradition sends him to Alexandria. He founded the Church there, the patriarchates of Alexandria still trace their succession to him, and he was killed by a hostile crowd on Easter Monday around the year 68 — dragged with a rope around his neck through the streets of the city to a place outside the walls. The Egyptian Christians buried him in a small church on the harbor.

Eight centuries later Venetian merchants stole the body from Alexandria and carried it to the lagoon city that has been Mark's adopted home ever since. The Lion of Venice is his.

> *"Take Mark, and bring him with you; for he is useful to me in ministry."* — Paul, *Second Letter to Timothy* 4:11
