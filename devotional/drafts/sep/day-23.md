## September 23

# Adamnan of Iona

*Abbot, biographer, peacemaker, c. 624–704*

He was born in Donegal a generation after Columba died, a kinsman of the Cenél Conaill — Columba's own people — and entered Iona as a young monk under the abbot Seghine, who had himself been trained by men who remembered the founder. He was elected ninth abbot of Iona in 679, in his middle fifties, and held the office for twenty-five years. The community he inherited was the head of a great federation of Columban houses spread from western Ireland to the Hebrides to Lindisfarne — the largest monastic network in the British Isles at the time — and the most pressing piece of unfinished business the founder had left him was the question of when to keep Easter. Iona kept the old Celtic computation; Rome and most of the rest of the church kept the newer Alexandrian calendar; the difference could put the date as much as a month apart. Adamnan, after a visit to Wearmouth-Jarrow in 686 where he met Bede's master Ceolfrid, became persuaded that the Roman date was correct. He spent the rest of his life trying, and largely failing, to bring his own community along. Iona did not adopt the Roman Easter until eleven years after his death.

He wrote two books that have outlasted the controversy. *De Locis Sanctis* — a careful guidebook to the holy places of the East, written down from the eyewitness account of a Frankish bishop named Arculf who had been shipwrecked on Iona on his way back from Jerusalem — became the standard pilgrim's reference of the early medieval church and was abridged by Bede a generation later. *Vita Sancti Columbae* — three books on the founder, drawn from the community's living memory and the records kept since Columba's death in 597 — is one of the most beautiful early-medieval saints' lives.

His other great work was political. In 697, at the synod of Birr in the Irish midlands, he gathered the bishops and kings of Ireland and persuaded them to enact what is still called *Cáin Adomnáin* — the Law of the Innocents — the first written legislation in Western Europe to forbid the killing of women, children, and clergy in war. He died on Iona in his eightieth year. The biography of Columba he had written, the law he had given Ireland, the Easter he could not quite carry: three works of patience, two of which lasted.

> *"In every conflict, spare the women, the children, and those who pray."* — *Cáin Adomnáin,* 697
