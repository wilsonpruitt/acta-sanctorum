## February 20

# Eleutherius of Tournai

*Bishop, born in exile, c. 456–531*

He was born at Blandain, two leagues outside Tournai, the year Childeric began pressing the Christian Gallo-Roman population out of the Scheldt valley. His parents were Christians of the *Menapii*, the Gallic-Romanized people who had held the country between the Scheldt and the North Sea since the late Republic. The family had been driven out of Tournai in the 480s when Clovis began his wars against the last Roman administration in northern Gaul. The boy grew up in the refugee community at Blandain. Saint Medard's mother was his cousin, and the two boys were companions.

The community at Blandain raised a small church to Saint Peter when Clovis had completed his conversion and the persecution relaxed; Eleutherius was ordained priest there in his early thirties. The diocese of Tournai had been vacant since the death of its first bishop Theodore in 500. After a year's vacancy the Christian community elected Eleutherius to succeed him, and he took the see at the beginning of 501.

He governed it for thirty years. The diocese was the largest in northern Gaul, running from the upper Scheldt across to the North Sea, and the Christian population at his consecration was perhaps one in ten. He spent three decades baptizing. The acts preserve the small details: he traveled on foot, kept perpetual fast in Lent and the Ember weeks, ordained eleven hundred priests and deacons by his own count in a letter to Pope Hormisdas, founded the cathedral chapter and its school, and wrote a now-lost catechetical treatise that Gregory of Tours had praised.

His most famous engagement was with the Arians of the upper Scheldt, who had begun pressing their teaching into the community he had spent twenty years bringing into the church. He answered with a Quadragesimal preaching tour from Tournai south to the Aisne, calling the Arian families back to Nicene communion. The tour worked. The Arian party at Tournai was substantially gone by his death.

He died on the twenty-fifth of February, 531, and was buried beside Medard at Blandain. The great Notre-Dame de Tournai that rose over the next millennium stands on the same foundations, his bones still under the high altar.

> *"Recede a peccato, et accede ad veritatem."* — Eleutherius, in the closing exhortation of every recorded Quadragesimal sermon: *withdraw from sin, and approach the truth*
