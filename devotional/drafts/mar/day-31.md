## March 31

# Benjamin the Deacon

*Persian martyr, d. 421*

He was a deacon of the Christian community in Persia — by the early fifth century a substantial Church, with bishops, monasteries, and three centuries of presence behind it — at a moment when the Sassanid king Bahram V was beginning a new persecution. The cause was political: the Christians of Persia were suspected of loyalty to the Roman Empire after the conversion of Constantine. The persecution was harsh.

Benjamin was arrested early. He was imprisoned for two years. At last a Roman envoy intervened on his behalf, and the king agreed to release him on one condition: that he never again preach the Christian faith publicly. The envoy accepted on Benjamin's behalf. Benjamin, when he learned of the bargain, broke it immediately. He went back to preaching the next day. The authorities re-arrested him.

The torture that followed is reported in detail by Sozomen, the church historian, who had reliable Persian sources. The executioners drove reeds under his fingernails and under the skin of his arms and legs. They drove a knotted stake through his abdomen. He was killed in stages over several days. He did not, the chronicler insists, recant; in the long intervals between the torments he prayed silently, and when he could, sang the psalter.

He had refused the bargain because there was nothing the king could give him that was worth the silence. He had only the gospel to give. He gave it for as long as he could speak it and a few minutes longer.

> *"A man who has known what light is cannot agree to walk in darkness."* — Benjamin, in the negotiation with the envoy
