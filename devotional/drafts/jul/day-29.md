## July 29

# Martha of Bethany

*Sister of Lazarus, hostess of Christ, first century*

She kept house. She had a sister Mary and a brother Lazarus and a house in the village of Bethany on the eastern slope of the Mount of Olives, fifteen furlongs from Jerusalem — a comfortable walk in the cool of the evening, far enough out of the city to be private but close enough to be present whenever the pilgrim feasts brought Galilean rabbis to the capital. The Gospel of John says Jesus loved them — *Iesus autem diligebat Martham et sororem eius Mariam et Lazarum* — and the village house in Bethany seems to have been, in the last year of his earthly ministry, the closest thing he had to a household of his own near Jerusalem.

Luke gives the famous afternoon. He came in with the disciples. Mary sat at his feet to listen. Martha was preparing the meal — *anxious and troubled about many things*, the Greek has — and finally went into the room and said it: *Lord, dost thou not care that my sister hath left me to serve alone?* He answered her gently. *Martha, Martha, thou art careful and troubled about many things; but one thing is needful, and Mary hath chosen that good part.* The exchange is sometimes read as a rebuke. The early commentators read it as a kindness: he had named her twice, by her own name, and had not told her to stop cooking, only to remember that the cooking was not the one thing. She did not, by the chronicler's careful inference, take it as rebuke. She did not stop serving him afterward.

John gives the harder afternoon. Their brother Lazarus had died, and the sisters had sent for Jesus, and Jesus had delayed two days. When he at last came up the road to Bethany, Martha went out to meet him — Mary stayed in the house — and said, *Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died.* She did not stop there. *But I know, that even now, whatsoever thou wilt ask of God, God will give it thee.* He told her he was the resurrection and the life. He asked her, *Believest thou this?* She gave the confession that Peter had given at Caesarea Philippi, in almost the same words: *Yea, Lord; I have believed that thou art the Christ, the Son of God, which should come into the world.* It is the only Johannine confession of the divinity of Christ delivered by a woman, and it is delivered before the raising of Lazarus, not after — by faith, not by sight.

She kept the house. She fed him the meal six days before the Passion when Mary anointed his feet with the spikenard. The Provençal tradition records that after the resurrection the three siblings landed by boat on the southern coast of Gaul, and that Martha founded a Christian community at Tarascon on the Rhône and died there in old age. The AASS prints both the *Vita* and the cautious Bollandist note that the journey is not historically attested.
