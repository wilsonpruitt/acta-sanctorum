## September 11

# Paphnutius of Egypt

*Bishop, confessor of Nicaea, c. 270–c. 360*

He had been a desert monk under Anthony the Great when Diocletian's persecution caught up with him, around the year 308. Maximinus Daia, ruling the eastern provinces, had ordered a new refinement of cruelty for those who would not sacrifice — they were not to be killed but disfigured. The right eye was put out with a sharpened iron; the tendons behind the left knee were severed with a hot blade so the man could never again walk straight. Then they were sent into the copper mines of Phaeno, at the edge of the Red Sea desert, to die slowly of work. Paphnutius was sent with the others. He survived. When Constantine published the Edict of Milan in 313 the survivors came back, hobbling, half-blind, and went back to the cells they had left.

He was elected bishop of an unknown see in the Upper Thebaid not long afterward. When the great council met at Nicaea in 325 he was carried there with the other Egyptian bishops — Athanasius was a deacon in his train — and Constantine, on first meeting him, recognized the empty socket and the dragging leg and bent down and kissed both. Rufinus and Socrates both preserve what happened next. The council was debating whether to require celibacy of all clergy — a proposal that had support from many of the bishops, especially from Spain. Paphnutius rose and spoke against it. *Marriage is honorable,* he said, *and the bed undefiled. Do not lay on others a yoke neither we nor our fathers were able to bear.* The Bollandists note that he had himself been celibate from his youth. The council heard him out and adjusted the canon: the church would receive into orders married men, and married priests would keep their wives.

He returned to Egypt, served as bishop into great age, helped Athanasius against the Arians, and died in his cell sometime around 360, eyepatch and limp and all. The man who had been disfigured for refusing to give up Christ spent the rest of his life keeping the church from disfiguring others.
