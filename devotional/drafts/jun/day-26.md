## June 26

# Anthelm of Belley

*Carthusian prior and bishop, 1107–1178*

He was born of the noble house of Chignin in Savoy, and was a canon of Geneva and provost of Belley — two comfortable benefices held simultaneously — when, at thirty, on a hunting trip in the Chartreuse mountains, he stopped at the Carthusian motherhouse founded by Bruno of Cologne. He stayed three days. He went home and arranged his affairs. He returned within months, asked for the habit, and was received as a novice at the *Grande Chartreuse*. He was made prior of the house at thirty-two, the seventh in succession to Bruno, and he governed it twenty years.

He rebuilt the *Grande Chartreuse* after an avalanche in 1132 had destroyed the original foundation. He turned the loose confederation of Carthusian houses — until then each a private foundation under its prior — into a unified order with regular General Chapters, the first in 1140. He drafted what would become the *Consuetudines* of the order, the customary that gave the Carthusians a constitutional form they have kept eight hundred years. He founded the women's Carthusian branch by accepting Beatrice of Ornacieux's Prébayon community in 1145. He retired from the priorate to be a simple monk again in 1152, was forced out by the pope in 1163 to take the see of Belley as bishop, and accepted only on the condition that he keep his Carthusian habit, his Carthusian fast, and a Carthusian cell — built for him in the cathedral close — to which he could retire each evening when the pastoral hours of the day were done.

He governed Belley fifteen years. He defended the rights of the Lyonnais clergy against the imperial party in the schism between Pope Alexander III and the antipopes of Frederick Barbarossa, even refusing the emperor's safe-conduct when summoned to the imperial diet. He intervened personally in the case of Count Humbert III of Savoy when the count had unjustly imprisoned a priest, and laid the count's lands under interdict until the priest was freed. He died at Belley in his cell, June 26, 1178. The Savoyards held him long after — until the Reformation — as the bishop who had refused to leave his Carthusian habit behind.

> *"Far be it from me to make a testament — I have never possessed anything I would reckon my own; it belonged to the Church which I governed."* — Anthelm, on his deathbed, refusing to make a will
