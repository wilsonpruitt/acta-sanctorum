## August 15

# Alypius of Tagaste

*Bishop, lifelong friend of Augustine, c. 360–c. 430*

He grew up two streets from Augustine in the small Numidian town of Tagaste, the younger of the two by a few years. He admired the older boy, followed him to Carthage to study rhetoric, then to Rome to study law. He had no idea where any of it would lead. He thought, like his teacher, that he was building a career. What he was building was a friendship that would carry both of them into the church.

Augustine, who could never be brief about Alypius, tells one early story against him with great affection. Alypius had sworn off the gladiatorial games as bloody and pointless. Friends in Rome dragged him to the amphitheater anyway. He shut his eyes; the crowd roared; he opened them. He was, the *Confessions* says, *no longer the man who had come, but a member of the crowd to which he had come.* The cure took years. It came in the same Milanese garden where Augustine wept under a fig tree and heard the child's voice say *take and read.* Augustine read Romans 13: *put on the Lord Jesus Christ.* He passed the book to Alypius. Alypius read the next verse: *him that is weak in the faith, receive ye.* He took it to himself. The two were baptized together by Ambrose at the next Easter vigil.

Alypius went home and became bishop of the town they had both left as boys. Augustine became bishop of Hippo, sixty miles north. They wrote each other constantly for the next forty years, fought Donatists and Pelagians shoulder to shoulder, grew old in adjacent sees. When Paulinus of Nola, hearing of Alypius from Augustine, asked for a written life of him, Augustine took on the task himself: *totum Alypium inseram præcordiis tuis* — *I shall set the whole of Alypius into your heart.* He never finished it. The *Confessions*, begun a few years later, carries Alypius only as far as the baptism. The rest of him survives in scattered letters, in the marginal anecdotes of a friend too busy keeping a province in the church to write the life he had promised.

The friendship outlasted the biography. Most do.

> *"Whoever thinks kindly of that man thinks of God's great mercy and God's wonderful gifts."* — Augustine, *Letter* 27 to Paulinus
