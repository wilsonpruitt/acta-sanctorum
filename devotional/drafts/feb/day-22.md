## February 22

# Margaret of Cortona

*Franciscan tertiary, 1247–1297*

She was born to a peasant family at Laviano in Tuscany; her mother died when she was seven; her stepmother was cruel; she ran away at seventeen with a young nobleman named Arsenio who had seen her on the road. He took her to his castle at Montepulciano. For nine years she lived there as his mistress and bore him a son. He never married her. He never quite refused to.

Late one autumn afternoon Arsenio's dog came back from a hunt without him and would not stop pulling at Margaret's skirt. She followed it into the woods. The dog led her to a clearing where Arsenio had been killed by his enemies and dumped in a covered pit. She uncovered him. The chronicle does not soften her response. She took the child by the hand, walked back to the castle, gave away every piece of jewelry and clothing Arsenio had given her, walked the twenty miles to her father's house, and asked to be taken in. Her stepmother refused. Her father, too weak to stand against her, refused. She walked, with her son, the next forty miles to Cortona, where she had heard the Franciscans took penitents.

The Franciscan friars Giovanni and Giunta examined her at the door, took her in, placed her in the care of two laywomen. She did three years of probation. She wore a hairshirt. She had wanted to be led through the city of Montepulciano with a halter around her neck as a public penitent; the friars refused her this, judging it more vanity than penance. She accepted the refusal. She gave the rest of her life to the Cortona poor: she founded the city's hospital for the destitute sick, *Santa Maria della Misericordia*, and worked in it daily for two decades.

She died at fifty, having spent twenty-three years undoing nine.

> *"My past, Lord, is a long road I can never walk back. Walk it with me forward."* — Margaret, in a recorded ecstasy
