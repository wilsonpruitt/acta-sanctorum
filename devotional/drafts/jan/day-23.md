## January 23

# John the Almsgiver

*Patriarch of Alexandria, c. 552–616*

He was a Cypriot, a widower, a layman, and over sixty when the emperor Heraclius asked him to take Alexandria. The Persians had just begun their march through Syria; Antioch had fallen; Jerusalem would fall within a few years; the Christian refugees pouring south into Egypt had no one to receive them. John accepted on one condition: he would call the poor his masters.

He kept books. He kept them very precisely. He had drawn up a list of all the destitute in Alexandria at the start of his patriarchate — *seven and a half thousand of my masters and brothers*, he said — and he marked it daily as families received bread and clothes from the patriarchal treasury. When the bishops of his synod objected that a patriarch should not be a clerk to beggars, John told them that the books in question were the books God would consult.

When Jerusalem fell to the Persians in 614, John sent shiploads of food and money to the besieged Christians and welcomed the refugees who arrived in his city by the thousands. When the church treasury ran dry, he melted down the plate. When a beggar tricked him three days running by appearing in different disguises and receiving alms each time, his stewards refused to give a fourth time. John called them back. *Give to him*, he said, *until we have tested whether he is Christ.*

He died at home in Cyprus, having returned there during a Persian raid on Alexandria, and was buried with the inscription he had asked for in advance: that he had given everything that was given him, and that this had been the only joy of his life.

> *"I will not be reconciled to God until I have made up to my brethren what I have failed to give them."* — John the Almsgiver
