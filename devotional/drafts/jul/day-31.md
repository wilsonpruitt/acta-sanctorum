## July 31

# Ignatius of Loyola

*Soldier, founder of the Society of Jesus, 1491–1556*

He was Íñigo López de Loyola, youngest of thirteen children of a minor Basque noble family, raised on a small castle-tower above Azpeitia. He was a page at the court of the treasurer of Castile, then a soldier in his twenties, ambitious, vain about his appearance and his swordsmanship, with two arrests for street brawling and an open litigation against an ecclesiastical court for tonsure-clipping a rival. He was thirty when, defending Pamplona against a French army in May 1521, a cannonball passed between his legs and shattered the right one. He was carried home to Loyola on a stretcher.

He spent six months in bed with the leg, then six more after the bone-setters re-broke it because it had healed crooked. He asked for books. The castle had two: the *Legenda Aurea* lives of the saints and a Spanish translation of Ludolph of Saxony's *Life of Christ*. He read both. He noticed that when he daydreamed about returning to the wars and his lady, he was elated while daydreaming and dry afterward; when he daydreamed about walking barefoot to Jerusalem like the saints he had been reading, he was elated while daydreaming and sweet afterward. He paid careful attention to the difference. It is the seed of the *Discernment of Spirits* he would later codify.

He left Loyola in spring 1522, hung his sword at the Black Madonna of Montserrat, and spent a year in a cave above Manresa in vigils, hunger, and the working-out of what would become the *Spiritual Exercises*. He walked to Jerusalem and back. He went to school in Spain at thirty-three to learn the Latin he had skipped as a boy, was twice arrested by the Inquisition for preaching without orders, and at last took his MA at Paris at forty-three. He gathered six companions there — Francis Xavier, Peter Faber, Diego Laínez, and three others. They walked together to Rome. Pope Paul III approved the Society of Jesus in 1540.

He governed the Society sixteen years from a small room in Rome on the Via degli Astalli — three windows, a writing table, a kneeling-stool. He sent Xavier to India and Japan, Faber to Germany, missionaries to Ethiopia, Brazil, the Philippines. He left, at his death, a Society of more than a thousand in twelve provinces across four continents. He died at Rome, July 31, 1556, of a long stomach illness; the brothers had not expected the death that morning, and he received no last rites — only a single word repeated as he could, *Jesús*.

> *"Take, Lord, and receive all my liberty, my memory, my understanding, and my entire will."* — Ignatius, *Suscipe* prayer, *Spiritual Exercises*
