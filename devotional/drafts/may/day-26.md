## May 26

# Augustine of Canterbury

*First archbishop of the English, d. 608*

He was prior of Gregory the Great's own monastery of Saint Andrew on the Caelian hill in Rome when, in 596, the pope picked him out of the community and sent him north with forty monks to convert the English. The mission was Gregory's long ambition. Years before, walking through the slave market in the Forum, the pope had seen flaxen-haired British boys for sale and asked their nation; told *Angli*, he is said to have answered, *Non Angli, sed angeli* — not Angles, but angels. He had wanted to go himself; called instead to be pope, he sent Augustine.

The party set out through Gaul, and at Angers the monks lost their courage. They had heard the English were ferocious and the British Channel rough; they sent Augustine back to Rome to ask permission to abandon the mission. Gregory wrote him a letter that did not give permission. He set out again. They landed on the Isle of Thanet in the spring of 597. Æthelberht of Kent, whose Frankish queen Bertha was a Christian and had her own chapel at Canterbury, came down to meet them — outdoors, lest the strangers' magic prevail under a roof. Augustine and the monks walked toward him singing, a silver cross before them, an icon of Christ on a board.

Æthelberht did not believe at once. He gave them a house in Canterbury and let them preach. Within months the king was baptized; by Christmas, the chronicler says, ten thousand of his people followed him. Augustine consecrated Mellitus for London and Justus for Rochester. He sent the famous *Responsiones* — nine practical questions on how to govern a young English church — back to Gregory, and treasured the answers. He met the British bishops in the west, failed to bring them under his authority, and let the failure stand. He died on May 26, 608, some years before his church had its first English-born bishop.

> *"The best message of everlasting joy we bring thee, O King: if thou receive it, here and in the eternal kingdom thou shalt be eternally beatified."* — Augustine, before Æthelberht at Thanet
