## April 24

# Mellitus of Canterbury

*Archbishop, d. 624*

He was a Roman abbot, perhaps of the monastery on the Caelian Hill that Gregory the Great had himself founded, when Gregory in 601 sent a second wave of missionaries to England to reinforce Augustine of Canterbury. Mellitus led them. He carried Gregory's famous instructions on what to do with the pagan temples — *do not destroy them, but cleanse them with holy water and rededicate the altars* — and a small library of liturgical books for the new English Church.

Augustine made him the first bishop of London. The Saxon king of Essex, Saeberht, had received baptism; his city of London was to be the new see. Mellitus served there for fifteen years. He built the first cathedral of Saint Paul, on the same hill where the present one still stands. He baptized many; he travelled to Gaul and Rome for synods; he was a diligent if not famous bishop. He was getting old.

When Saeberht died in 616, his three pagan sons inherited the kingdom and the new bishop. They came to a service and demanded the Eucharist from Mellitus, although they had not been baptized. He refused. They drove him out of the kingdom. He took ship for Gaul, intending to retire to a monastery on the continent, but the bishops of Canterbury called him back when the political situation eased. He returned to find that Justus had been made archbishop in his absence; when Justus died in 619, Mellitus was elected to succeed him.

He served as archbishop of Canterbury for the last five years of his life. He was old by then; he suffered terribly from gout; he could not always travel. The famous fire-quenching story Bede preserves comes from his last years: Canterbury caught fire, the wind drove the flames toward the cathedral, and the monks carried Mellitus on a stretcher into the path of the blaze. He prayed. The wind changed.

He died on April 24, 624, in the cathedral he had saved.

> *"The Lord, who has carried me through many fires, will carry me through this one too."* — Mellitus, before the fire was set against him
