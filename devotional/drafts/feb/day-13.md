## February 13

# Modomnoc of Ossory

*Irish bishop, sixth century*

He was an Irish nobleman of the line of Niall of the Nine Hostages, sent in his youth to Wales to study at the great monastic school of David at Mynyw. He stayed there ten years. He took his turn at all the labors of the house — the strict ones, of cold water and barley bread and silence — and was given, at last, the charge of the abbey beehives. He proved unusually good at the work. The chronicles say the bees grew accustomed to him, that they swarmed without stinging him, that they followed him when he walked.

When he had finished his training and David sent him home to evangelize his own people, Modomnoc went down to the harbor with the small boat his abbot had given him. He had loaded his books and his few possessions. As the boat began to pull away from the Pembrokeshire coast, the entire colony of bees from the abbey hives rose up in a cloud and followed him out over the water. Modomnoc, alarmed at having stolen the abbey's livelihood, turned the boat around and rowed back to give the bees up. David, smiling, told him to keep them. *They have chosen you*, he said. *They will be the first Christians among the Irish bees.*

The bees crossed the Irish Sea with him. He landed on the coast of what would later be the kingdom of Ossory, in Leinster, and set them up in a hive at his hermitage there. He was eventually made bishop of Ossory. He preached, baptized, founded small churches in the woods, kept the hive. He died at an advanced age.

The Irish, who appreciate this kind of saint, still call him the patron of beekeepers. Welsh tradition holds that every Irish bee descends from a Welsh queen who once chose Modomnoc.
