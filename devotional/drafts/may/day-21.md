## May 21

# Godric of Finchale

*English hermit, c. 1065–1170*

He was born in Norfolk to poor parents, Ailward and Ædwen — Christians of no rank but of plain faith. The boy had no schooling. Galfridus, who wrote his Vita from the longer one by Reginald of Durham, calls him *rusticanus et idiota nihilque sciens nisi Christum Jesum, et hunc crucifixum* — a peasant and unlettered, knowing nothing save Christ Jesus and him crucified. He turned to trade, spending sixteen years as a merchant, walking the villages of eastern England with packs and working the small coastal shipping. A near-drowning at Wellestrem — wading after a beached dolphin while the tide passed his ankles, his knees, then over his head — became the moment he first applied the prophet's word *the deep encompassed me*, and began to think of another road.

He gave up trade in his late thirties for a pilgrim's life. He went to Rome and Jerusalem barefoot, in great poverty. He came back to England looking for a place to serve God alone, and was told in a dream to seek out Finchale — a wooded ground on a bend of the Wear not far from Durham. He found it after long searching. He lived there first with a sister, *cum sorore paupercula*; after she died he lived alone for the rest of his very long life.

The plain at Finchale was small. He dug with a hoe, ate what he grew, gave whatever was left to those who came. The monks of Durham sent an older brother to instruct him and bring him the Sacrament. He built a small oratory in honor of John the Baptist. He lived sixty years a hermit. Galfridus, who as a boy met the old man in the last weeks before his death, gives the only physical description AASS keeps of him: small in body, the belly thinned by fasting, the hair white, the face long, the eyes pale. *Velox ad audiendum, tardus autem ad loquendum* — quick to hear, slow to speak. He could weep with those who wept; he did not know how to laugh with those who laughed.

He died May 21, 1170, in his oratory, beside the altar where he had been used to lie prostrate. The same patch of floor took his grave.

> *"Sainte Marie virgine, moder Iesu Cristes Nazarene, onfo, schild, help thin Godric."* — Godric's own Middle English hymn, taken down by Reginald of Durham
