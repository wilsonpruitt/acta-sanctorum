## February 24

# Matthias the Apostle

*Apostle, replacement for Judas, first century*

He appears in Scripture in nine verses. After the Ascension, Peter stood up in the upper room — among the hundred and twenty who had gathered to wait for Pentecost — and proposed that the betrayer's place be filled. The replacement had to be a man who had been with them *from the baptism of John until the day when he was taken up from us*. Two men met the criterion: Joseph called Barsabbas, surnamed Justus, and Matthias. The apostles prayed; they cast lots; the lot fell on Matthias. He took his place among the Twelve.

Almost everything else about him is tradition. He preached, the early historians say, in Judea first, then north into Cappadocia, then east into Colchis along the Black Sea — what is now Georgia. The Caucasus mountains have an ancient memory of him; the Georgian Church still claims him as their first apostle. The accounts of his death vary. Some say he was stoned at Jerusalem and beheaded for good measure; some say he was crucified in Colchis; the Coptic tradition has him perish in the Roman colonies of North Africa. The Bollandists in their Februarius volume gather all these and admit they cannot reconcile them.

What is certain is the moment of his selection. The Twelve had been broken by one of their own; the gospel was about to be carried out to the world; the gap had to be filled before Pentecost. Matthias filled it. He did not boast. He did not refuse. The lot fell on him and he stood up. The Church has remembered him ever since as the man who was willing to step into a vacancy that a friend had created by his own betrayal.

That, too, is a vocation. Every Church on earth has its quietly replaced Judases, its men and women who took the empty chair without saying a word.

> *"Show us which of these two you have chosen."* — the prayer of the apostles, before the casting of lots (Acts 1:24)
