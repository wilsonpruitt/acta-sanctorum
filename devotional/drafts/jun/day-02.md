## June 2

# The Martyrs of Lyons

*Forty-eight Christians of Lyons and Vienne, 177*

A summer mob in the colonial Roman city of Lugdunum — the modern Lyons — in the seventeenth year of Marcus Aurelius drove the Christians of the place out of the marketplace, the baths, the public houses. They beat them in the streets. They drove them indoors. When the governor returned to the city he ordered the Christians arrested, and the prison filled with some forty-eight souls, men and women, Gallic and Greek, of every rank. The bishop Pothinus, then in his ninety-first year, was carried before the tribunal half-dead from the beatings he had already taken, and answered the governor's *Who is the God of the Christians?* with *If thou art worthy, thou shalt know.* They returned him to the prison; he died in it within two days.

The household slave Blandina was a small, frail woman whose mistress feared she would not stand the questioning. She was hung on a stake, the chronicler says, in the form of a cross, in the amphitheater, with wild beasts loosed upon her; the beasts would not touch her. She was taken down and brought back the next day. The boy Ponticus, fifteen years old, was set beside her to watch each other; she encouraged him to the end, like a mother, the chronicler says, sending her child before her to the king. When the boy was dead they put her last into a net, and a bull was loosed upon her. She had asked, as her only request, to be the last in the dying.

The community wrote the letter the church now possesses — the *Letter of the Churches of Lyons and Vienne* — to the churches of Asia and Phrygia, who had sent missionaries to Gaul a generation before. It described what had happened with extraordinary care. It refused to call the dead *martyrs*; only Christ, they said, was the true Witness, and the rest were *confessors*, those who had not yet finished their confession. It begged the Eastern churches to pray that they would. They did finish. The amphitheater is still there, on the slope above the rivers. The letter has lasted longer than the stones.

> *"If thou art worthy, thou shalt know."* — Pothinus to the governor of Lyons
