## July 7

# Willibald of Eichstätt

*West Saxon bishop and pilgrim, c. 700–787*

He was a Wessex boy of a noble family, kin to Boniface of Mainz, given as a child of three to the abbey of Bishop's Waltham in Hampshire after a near-fatal illness. He took the habit at twenty. In his early twenties he persuaded his father Richard and his brother Wynnebald to set out with him on the long pilgrimage East. They left Hamble in 720 and walked across France in winter. The father died at Lucca in northern Italy and was buried in the church there. Willibald and his brother went on to Rome, prayed at the apostles' tombs, and then — alone of the Anglo-Saxon pilgrims of that decade — kept going east.

He spent seven years in the Holy Land. He sailed from Naples to Sicily, from Sicily to Ephesus, from Cyprus to Tartus on the Syrian coast. He was arrested at Emesa as a suspected Frankish spy and held for some weeks until a Spanish Muslim merchant who had known his face in Naples vouched for him. He walked to Damascus, to Nazareth, to Capernaum, to the Mount of Olives. He drank from Jacob's well at Sychar. He bought a small amount of frankincense in the Bethlehem market and smuggled it back to Europe in a hollowed gourd through the customs at Tyre — knowing, the chronicler says, that there was no customs duty on a gourd full of nothing. The *Hodoeporicon* in which his cousin Hugeburc of Heidenheim recorded these journeys is the first travel narrative by an Englishman to survive.

He returned through Constantinople and paused two years at Monte Cassino before Boniface called him on to Germany. Boniface ordained him priest in 740 and consecrated him bishop of the new see of Eichstätt in Bavaria the following year. He governed Eichstätt forty-five years. He founded the double monastery of Heidenheim, where his brother Wynnebald and his sister Walpurga ran it as abbot and abbess; he organized the parish system of his territory; he survived the death of Boniface in 754 and the upheavals of Pippin's accession.

He died at Eichstätt, July 7, 787, having outlived almost everyone he had set out with. The frankincense, the chronicler says, lasted his whole life.

> *"It is light to carry what is bought for thanksgiving."* — Willibald, on his gourd
