## November 4

# Emeric (Imre) of Hungary

*Crown prince of the Magyars, son of Stephen, d. 1031*

He was the only surviving son of Stephen of Hungary — the king who had taken the Christian crown from Pope Sylvester II at the dawn of the new millennium — and his Bavarian wife Gisela, sister of the emperor Henry II. He was born around 1007, named for his uncle the emperor (the Hungarian *Imre* is the Magyar form of the German *Heinrich*), and raised at the new Christian court at Esztergom in the strict piety his father had imported from the Bavarian and Lotharingian missionary houses. The tutor Stephen chose for him was Gerard of Csanád, the Venetian Benedictine bishop eventually killed by the pagan revolt of 1046. The *Vita Maior Sancti Stephani* preserves the program of the boy's education: the seven liberal arts in the morning, the *Rule of Saint Benedict* in the afternoon, the *Imitatio* of the Eastern desert fathers in the evening. The prince was being raised to be the second Christian king of Hungary.

He was being raised with the assumption that the throne was his and would come to him in middle life. Stephen, by 1030, was sixty years old; the kingdom was secure; the succession was clear; the long work of converting Hungary from a pagan tribal confederation into a Christian European kingdom was at the point where it could finally be turned over to the next generation. Imre had been knighted, betrothed, and was preparing in the autumn of 1031 to assume the regency of the southern Croatian-Serbian frontier when, on the second of September, he was killed by a wild boar in a hunting accident in the forests of the Bihar mountains. He was twenty-four years old.

The shock to his father was the central wound of the rest of Stephen's life. The *Vita* preserves the king's prayer: *Lord, you have taken from me what you gave me; what shall I give you back?* The answer, as Stephen formed it over the next seven years of his life — he died in 1038 — was the dedication of the kingdom itself. With no heir, with the next of his line a cousin who could not be trusted with the crown, with the Christian settlement of Hungary still fragile, Stephen lifted the crown of his kingdom on his own deathbed and gave it to the Virgin Mary as her perpetual fief: *Regnum Marianum,* the Marian kingdom, Hungary as Mary's land. The dedication held. The Hungarian nation has called itself by that name ever since.

What survives of Imre is small. The *Vita* preserves his vow of perpetual chastity, taken on his wedding night with his bride's consent. It preserves his nightly reading of the psalter, his almsgiving to the poor of Esztergom, his unfailing presence at his father's morning Mass, the gentleness with which he treated the small Christian community that was just beginning to take root in his future kingdom. He was canonized in 1083 by Pope Gregory VII along with his father and his tutor Gerard — three saints in one family in one generation, the only such triple canonization in medieval church history. The young man who had been preparing to govern the kingdom became, in dying before he could, the cause of its dedication to Mary. The father had given the kingdom away because the son had gone before him.
