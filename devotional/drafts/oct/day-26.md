## October 26

# Cedd of Lastingham

*Northumbrian missionary bishop, brother of Chad, d. 664*

He was the eldest of four brothers raised at the monastic school on Lindisfarne under Aidan in the 640s — Cedd, Cynebill, Caelin, Chad — all four of them eventually ordained, all four of them missionary priests, all four in the front line of the Bernician evangelization of the next generation. Bede, who had grown up in the monastery Cedd's brother Chad would later govern at Lastingham, gives the family the warm portrait of book three of the *Historia Ecclesiastica.*

He was sent south in 653 at the request of King Peada of the Middle Angles, who had married a Northumbrian Christian princess and wanted missionaries for his country. He worked the South Yorkshire and Derbyshire moorlands for two years; Penda's death at Winwaed in 655 ended the period of pagan Mercian dominance and opened the south for the gospel; Cedd was reassigned to the East Saxons in 656 at the request of King Sigeberht the Good. He spent the next eight years founding the church of Essex from scratch.

He worked from a small wooden chapel built into the ruined Roman fortress of *Othona* on the Blackwater estuary — the *Saxon shore* fort the Romans had built in the third century to guard against North Sea raiders, abandoned for two hundred years, the long sea-walls still standing. The chapel he raised on the foundations of the fortress chapel — the chapel of *Saint-Peter-on-the-Wall,* perhaps the oldest still-standing church in England — is still there, the Roman tile and stone of its walls visible in the seventh-century masonry. From Bradwell-on-Sea he ordained priests, built churches up the rivers, organized a small Essex diocese, and went home each summer to Lindisfarne for the long northern monastic chapter.

In 664, returning from the Synod of Whitby — at which he had been one of the conciliators between the Roman and the Celtic Easter parties — he caught the plague that was sweeping the country and died at his own foundation at Lastingham on the North York Moors. His brother Chad came down to bury him and then stayed on as the new abbot. Bede preserves the small detail that Cedd had founded Lastingham on what had been a pagan burial ground; the brother had cleansed it with forty days of prayer and fasting before laying the foundation. The cleansing held. The abbey lasted four centuries. The chapel at Bradwell-on-Sea, looking out over the cold North Sea where it has stood for thirteen hundred years, has not had to be rebuilt.

> *"Locus iste sanctus est, in nomine Domini factus."* — *the Lastingham foundation prayer Bede preserves of Cedd*
