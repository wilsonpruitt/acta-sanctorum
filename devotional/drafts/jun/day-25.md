## June 25

# Prosper of Reggio Emilia

*Bishop, d. c. 466*

For centuries the calendars confused him with another man. There was a Prosper of Aquitaine — the lay theologian, the secretary of Pope Leo, the writer whose phrases the whole Latin Church still quotes — and the copyists, finding a Saint Prosper on the twenty-fifth of June, assumed the two were one. The Bollandists, sifting the day, untangled them: the Prosper the *Acta* keep here is no theologian and no Aquitanian, but the bishop of a small city in the Emilian plain, between Parma and Modena, on the left bank of the Crostolo — a man the world had nearly read out of his own life and handed to someone more famous.

He was made bishop of Reggio, the lessons say, unwilling and resisting. He had not wanted the chair. Once in it, he governed by a single rule, the one his biographer keeps as the whole of his character: he asked nothing of his people that he had not first done himself. He gave his nights to vigils and prayer and the hard usage of his own body, and his days to the poor, the orphan, and the widow. He wrote letter after letter to the bishops of the neighboring cities, urging them to the same. He built a church outside the walls and dedicated it to Saint Apollinaris, not knowing he was building his own tomb.

He died blessing them. The biographer holds the image still: the old bishop with his eyes and his hands lifted to heaven, the crowd beneath them, the blessing coming down. They buried him in the church he had raised, and the miracles began there. Long after, a king moved his bones into the city, and a countess raised a great abbey over them — honor enough, in the end, for a man who had asked for none of it, and whom the calendars had once tried to give away.

> *He proposed nothing to be done by his people which he had not first fulfilled in his own deed.* — from the Lessons of his Life
