## June 28

# Irenaeus of Lyons

*Bishop, theologian of the Tradition, c. 130–202*

He was born at Smyrna in Asia Minor, perhaps around 130, of a Greek-speaking Christian household, and as a boy he sat at the feet of the old bishop Polycarp — who had himself sat at the feet of the apostle John. Irenaeus would write, fifty years later, that he could still see the very chair Polycarp used, could recall the way the old man held his hands when he spoke, could repeat what Polycarp had said John had said about the Lord. The whole apostolic chain, in his own mind, was the length of two human lives.

He went west as a missionary, perhaps in his twenties, perhaps with the migration of Greek-speaking traders that established Christian communities along the Rhône valley. He was a priest of Lyons when the persecution of 177 fell, and was on a mission to Rome carrying letters from the prison cell of his bishop Pothinus when the killings happened in his absence. He returned to find his bishop dead, his community decimated, and himself elected to the empty see. He governed Lyons twenty-five years.

He wrote, in those years, the five books *Against Heresies* — the *Adversus Haereses* — which was the first great defense of the apostolic faith against the Gnostic systems then proliferating in the East. He took the Valentinians, the Marcionites, the Carpocratians, the Basilideans system by system, laid them out, refuted them not by speculation but by appeal to two things: the Scriptures, and the chain of bishops who had received the faith from the apostles. He gave the church the doctrine of *recapitulation* — that Christ had summed up all of human history in himself, undoing in his own life what Adam had done — that would run through every later Latin theology of redemption. He preserved the texts of Polycarp and of the *Letter of Clement*. He pleaded with Pope Victor in 190 not to excommunicate the Asian churches over the date of Easter, on the ground that diversity in matters not of faith was not a sin against unity.

He died at Lyons about 202, perhaps a martyr in the persecution of Septimius Severus, perhaps of natural causes — the early sources differ. He had been the first Christian to think in terms of *Tradition*, and the church has never stopped thinking that way since.

> *"The glory of God is a living man; and the life of man consists in beholding God."* — Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* IV.20.7
