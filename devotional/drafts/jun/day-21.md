## June 21

# Alban of Mainz

*Greek martyr in Germany, c. 404*

He was, by the elogium that Rabanus Maurus preserved in his ninth-century *Martyrologium*, a Greek of the Cyclades — most likely Naxos, the AASS reasons. He was a younger cleric in the church there, traveling under the older Theonestus and a third companion named Ursus. They sailed west to Milan in the late fourth century, came under the influence of Ambrose — then fighting the Arian dukes for control of the basilicas of the city — and were dispatched by the archbishop north into Gaul.

Ursus did not survive the journey. He was martyred at Aosta — *Augusta Prætoria in Salassiis* — and is commemorated on February 1. Theonestus and Alban kept walking, took the Rhine road, and reached Mainz. The Rhineland was a difficult mission. Bishop Aureus of Mainz had been deposed and exiled seven times by Arian factions in his decades on the chair; Theonestus arrived to a bishop in office only by intervals.

Alban was the younger of the two, the AASS says plainly: *junior, sic et ad lacessendos hæreticos animosior quam Theonestus, magistro suo præripuerit coronam martyrii* — younger, and more spirited in confronting the heretics than his master Theonestus, so that he ran ahead of his master to the crown of martyrdom. He preached in the streets of Mainz against the Arian faction. An *infida turba* — a faithless crowd — caught him in the open and beheaded him on the spot. The local Christians took his body and buried him in a small chapel they built on the place. The Romanesque verses still inscribed at the chapel in the Petersplatz date the death precisely: *Orbe quater centum numerante et quattuor annos, dum tenet Imperii moderamina Honorius* — when the world counted four hundred years and four, under the rule of Honorius. The year 404.

The Mainz tradition gave him afterward the cephalophore's iconography, pictured carrying his own severed head to the place of burial — a standard convention for beheaded martyrs, the AASS gently notes, and not a separate miracle. What was real was simpler: a Greek-speaking priest from a small island, sent west by Ambrose, killed on a Rhineland street for refusing to be quiet about the consubstantial Son.

> *"Albanus sanctus hic requievit, scisso cum capite agonem complevit."* — Saint Alban here rested, his contest finished with his head sundered. — verses on the Mainz chapel, AASS Junii IV
