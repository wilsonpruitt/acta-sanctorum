## May 16

# Brendan the Navigator

*Abbot of Clonfert, c. 480–577*

Everyone knows the voyage. Brendan and his monks in a hide-covered boat, seven years on the western ocean: the Mass said on the back of a whale, the island of birds, the column of crystal in the sea, the mountain of fire, and at the end of the water the Land of Promise of the Saints. It is one of the great stories of the Middle Ages. The Bollandists, who had many manuscripts of it in front of them, cut nearly the whole of it out of their edition — "apocryphal deliriums," they wrote, "prodigious fables" — and then grieved that the fable had so overgrown the man that his real holiness could barely be seen beneath it.

Under the legend is a quieter life. He was born in western Munster, son of Finlogh, of the line of Eugenius, his birth (the Irish said) foretold by Patrick. At a year old he was given to Saint Ita to foster, and she kept him five years and loved him; when she asked the child once what made him glad, he told her, in his childish speech, that he could see her and the other holy women holding him in their hands. He went on to Bishop Erc to read, and to Jarlath in Connaught to drink the cups of doctrine, and was ordained. He wrote a monastic Rule his sons believed an angel had dictated. He was father, in the end, to nearly three thousand monks across Ireland, and built his great house at Clonfert on the Shannon about the year 532.

He died at Annaghdown, in his sister Briga's monastery, an old man of ninety-odd, and was carried back to be buried in his own Clonfert. He had searched, the legend says, the whole face of the sea for the country of the saints. The truth the Bollandists kept is smaller and surer: a boy who had seen heaven in a nun's arms, and spent ninety years building places where other men might learn to see it too.

> *With vigils and fasts he wore down his body, and the greatest part of his time he spent in prayer; many he ruled under a holy discipline, and having stirred them to faith and piety, by many famous miracles he passed into heaven.* — Breviary of Coutances
