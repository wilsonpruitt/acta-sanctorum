## June 14

# Methodius I of Constantinople

*Patriarch and confessor of the icons, c. 788–847*

He was born at Syracuse in Sicily, of a wealthy Greek family long settled on the island, and went to Constantinople in his twenties expecting a court career. The court rejected him. He turned aside, took the monastic habit at Chenolakkos in Bithynia, and was made abbot before he was thirty. He was sent to Rome in 815 as the patriarch Nikephoros's legate in the protest against Leo V's renewal of iconoclasm. The mission failed. He was in Rome eight years, learning Latin, copying Greek manuscripts on the Aventine, and waiting for an empire that did not want him back.

He returned to Constantinople in 821, when Leo V was dead, expecting a reconciled church. He found instead the new emperor Michael II refusing to reopen the question. Methodius — a private monk, of no rank — wrote and circulated a defense of the icons addressed to the emperor. He was scourged, branded on the face, and thrown into a dungeon on the island of Saint Andrew in the Sea of Marmara, where he was kept seven years with two thieves. One of his fellow prisoners died in the cell, and Methodius endured the stench of the corpse without complaint. When at last he was let out, his face bore the marks of his torture: later Greek chroniclers report that his jaw and teeth had been so battered that he bound his cheeks ever after with a thin linen band — said to be the origin of the linen bands later Greek patriarchs wore.

He was made patriarch in March 843 by the empress Theodora, regent for the boy emperor Michael III, on the condition that he restore the icons. He convened a synod within days. He led the procession from the Blachernae church to Hagia Sophia on the first Sunday of Lent, 843 — the *Sunday of Orthodoxy* the Greek church has kept every year since — carrying icons through immense crowds who had not seen them in public for nearly thirty years. He governed four years more, dealing severely with the most rigorist of the iconodules as well as with the residual iconoclasts. He died at the patriarchal palace, June 14, 847, with the linen band still over his jaw and the synodikon of Orthodoxy he had drafted on the table.

> *"We, O fathers and brethren — both I myself and all of you — ought to give public thanks to Christ God, the giver of good things, that He has now at last bestowed liberty upon us, who for nearly thirty years were oppressed by the most savage yoke of heresy."* — Methodius, his address at his enthronement
