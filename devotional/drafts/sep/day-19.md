## September 19

# Theodore of Tarsus

*Greek monk, archbishop of Canterbury, organizer of the English church, c. 602–690*

He was born at Tarsus in Cilicia — Saint Paul's city — and grew up bilingual in Greek and Syriac, perhaps trilingual if one counts the Latin he picked up later. He studied at Antioch and Constantinople in the 620s, fled the Arab conquest of his homeland in the 640s, and ended up in Rome as an old man, a learned Greek monk in the Asian community on the Aventine, sixty-five years old and expecting nothing further from his life.

In 667 the church at Canterbury, two years bereft of an archbishop after the death of Wighard, asked Pope Vitalian for a candidate. Vitalian asked Hadrian, an African abbot whom the pope had wanted for the post. Hadrian declined and pointed at Theodore. The pope, after consulting the candidate's age and consenting, tonsured him in the Roman fashion (he had worn the Eastern monastic tonsure), waited four months for the hair to grow back, ordained him bishop, and put him on a ship for England.

He was sixty-eight when he stepped off at Canterbury in May 669. He would live another twenty-two years and reorganize the church of an entire kingdom. Bede, who was a child when Theodore died and grew up with men who had known him, gives him the warmest portrait in the *Ecclesiastical History.* He was, Bede writes, *the first archbishop to whom the whole English church consented to give its hand.* He toured every kingdom; he settled the date of Easter once and for all in Northumbria; he divided the over-large dioceses (this is what put him at odds with Wilfrid of York for the rest of his life); he convened the first English national council at Hertford in 672, where the bishops of seven kingdoms sat down together for the first time and signed nine canons that would govern the church for centuries.

The school he and Hadrian opened at Canterbury — Greek, Latin, computus, music, astronomy, scripture — produced a generation of English scholars whose Latin Bede still envied. He died at Canterbury at eighty-eight, on a September morning in 690. The English church he found in eight separate dioceses he left as one. It has held that shape since.

> *"The first archbishop to whom the whole English church consented to give its hand."* — Bede, *Historia Ecclesiastica*
