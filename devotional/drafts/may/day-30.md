## May 30

# Isaac the Anchorite of Constantinople

*Confessor, abbot, d. 383*

He came from Asia Minor and had borne, the Synaxaria say, the yoke of the Lord from his boyhood. He had been an ascetic in the eastern hill country for years when, in the spring of 378, he made his way to Constantinople. The emperor Valens — a hard Arian who had filled the orthodox sees of the East with men of his own party and exiled the Nicene confessors — was at that moment leading his army westward to meet the Gothic confederation that had crossed the Danube. Isaac broke through the imperial guard, took hold of the bridle of the emperor's horse, and would not let go.

He addressed the emperor — Theodoret preserves the words at *Historia Ecclesiastica* IV.34: *Where dost thou go, Emperor, thou that hast made war on God and hast him not for thy helper? It is he who has stirred the barbarians against thee, because thou didst expel from the churches those who praised him. Restore the best Pastors to their flocks, and thou shalt take a bloodless victory. But if thou go forward, neither shalt thou return, and thou shalt lose thine army.* Valens, in anger: *I will return, and I will kill thee.* Isaac, unafraid: *Kill me, if a lie be found in my words.*

The emperor had him thrown in prison and rode west. He did not return. The army was destroyed at Adrianople on the 9th of August, 378; Valens, wounded, fled to a barn where the Gothic *foederati* did not know he was hiding, and burned alive when the barn was fired. Isaac, the AASS records from Theophanes, perceived from his cell the smell of the burning emperor's body before any messenger had arrived in the city, and announced the death to those who stood with him.

The new Augustus Theodosius freed him within months. The monastic community that gathered around him became, under his successor Dalmatus, the *Dalmatorum monasterium* — the chief monastery of Constantinople for the next four centuries. He died *plenus dierum*, old and full of days, and the Greek Synaxaria keep his name on May 30. He is in the *Acta* a *Confessor* — not for blood, but for the prison.
