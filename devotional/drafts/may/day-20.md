## May 20

# Bernardine of Siena

*Franciscan preacher of the Holy Name, 1380–1444*

He was orphaned at six, raised by an aunt in Siena, and was studying canon law at seventeen when the plague returned. The hospital of Santa Maria della Scala lost most of its staff in a fortnight. Bernardine gathered a small band of fellow students and walked into the wards. For four months they nursed the dying, washed the dead, and read the Office over the pits when there were no priests left to read it. Both of his closest friends died beside him. When the plague had passed, he gave away what he had inherited, took the Franciscan habit of the Observance, and was sent into the hills to learn silence.

He came down twenty years later as the most powerful preacher in Italy. He spoke in piazzas because no church could hold the crowds — in Padua, in Milan, in Florence, in Rome, sometimes for three and four hours together, sometimes for eight successive days from the same square. He carried with him a wooden tablet on which he had painted the letters *IHS* — the Greek monogram of the Holy Name — surrounded by golden rays, and at the close of every sermon he raised it. Men set down their daggers; rival families embraced; the bonfires of vanities he kindled with the listeners' own ornaments became proverbial. He was three times offered a bishopric — Siena, Ferrara, Urbino — and three times refused, saying he had been sent to walk.

He died on the road, as he had asked, at L'Aquila in the Abruzzi on the vigil of the Ascension, 1444, having preached fifty sermons in the city in the last three weeks of his life. He was sixty-three. The painters of the next century gave him a hollowed face, a hand raised over the tablet, the three mitres he had refused at his feet. He had spent his strength on one word.

> *"On the same day I entered the religion of Francis, on the same day I was professed, on the same day I sang my first Mass and preached my first sermon; on such a day too I wish to pass from this life."* — Bernardine, preaching
