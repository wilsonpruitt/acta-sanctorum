## July 3

# Leo II, Pope

*Sicilian-born pontiff and ratifier of Constantinople III, d. 683*

He was born in Sicily, of a notary family of Aidone in the Greek-speaking interior of the island, in the years when Sicily was the breadbasket of the Eastern Empire and the great refuge of Greek monks fleeing the Arab conquests of the East. He was bilingual in Latin and Greek. He was, the chronicler in the *Liber Pontificalis* says, *eruditus in cantilena*, deeply learned in chant — a singer of remarkable skill who could improvise the modes of the Roman office and the Eastern liturgies alike. He was elected pope on the death of Agatho in January 681, but the imperial confirmation from Constantinople took eighteen months to arrive, and he was not consecrated until August 682. He governed only eleven months before he died.

His pontificate was short and substantive. He ratified the acts of the Sixth Ecumenical Council, the Council of Constantinople III, which had condemned the Monothelite heresy at the end of 681 — the heresy that Christ had only one will. The Latin reception of those acts was Leo's principal work. He sent the council's decrees to the bishops of Spain, to the king of the Visigoths, to the patriarchs of Antioch and Alexandria. He had to deal personally with the difficult question of his predecessor Honorius I, who had been anathematized at the council for negligently entertaining the Monothelite formula in a private letter. Leo's letter to the Spanish bishops on the matter is careful, learned, and pastoral: Honorius is condemned not for heresy but *quia non, ut decuit, apostolicam praedicationem confirmavit* — because he did not, as he should have, confirm the apostolic preaching with his own teaching. The distinction has lasted.

He composed several hymns for the Roman Office. He revised the chant of certain antiphons of the Holy Week. He kept up the Sicilian habit of personal almsgiving — the Lateran register notes weekly distributions to the Greek monasteries of Rome that he had instituted in his short tenure. He died June 28, 683, having held the see less than a year. The Roman calendar moved his feast to July 3 — the AASS day — to leave clear the apostles' feast days of late June. He had been pope long enough to settle the Latin reception of an ecumenical council, and not long enough for anything else.
