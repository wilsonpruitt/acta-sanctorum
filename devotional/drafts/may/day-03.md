## May 3

# Juvenal of Narni

*First bishop of Narni, c. 369–376*

He was an African by birth and a physician by training, a presbyter who had come up to Rome and lodged with a Christian woman of the imperial house. When he learned that the Umbrian city of Narni — thick with temples to Jove and Janus and Hercules and Saturn — had no bishop and almost no Christians, he sought out the one Christian family in the town, the Anicii, had them petition the pope, and was sent back as the city's first bishop, into a place that had not asked for one.

The conversion came through an act of violence that turned on the man who began it. Walking to his little oratory one day, Juvenal was seized near the temple of Liber Pater by a pagan who forced the hilt of a sword into the bishop's mouth to make him swallow meat offered to idols. Juvenal clenched it in his teeth and would not. The man, wrenching the blade back, cut his own throat and fell dead in the street. The crowd that had gathered to watch a bishop humiliated saw instead the bishop standing and the blasphemer dead, and nearly two thousand of them asked for baptism that Easter. When an army besieged the city in his fifth year, he walked the walls chanting the psalm, and a storm out of a clear noon drowned the besiegers at the temple of Hercules.

He governed seven years and died in peace — the oldest martyrologies call him only Confessor, though Gregory the Great, two centuries later, would give him the higher title of martyr for the throat he had nearly lost. They buried him on the Flaminian Way. The small crystal chalice he had carried to Narni — the one the biographer says never emptied, however many thousands drank from it — was still shown on his feast, long after the hand that poured from it was dust.

> *While so many thousands drank from it with their lips, the chalice was not refilled by the minister, but the holy libations grew in it.* — from his Life
