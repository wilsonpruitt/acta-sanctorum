## April 18

# Apollonius the Apologist

*Roman senator, martyr, c. 130s–185*

He was a Roman senator of philosophical bent, a Stoic in his early manhood and a Christian in his middle years, when his household slave Severus denounced him to the urban authorities around the year 185. Under Marcus Aurelius and now under Commodus, the imperial position on Christianity was ambiguous: the religion was illegal but the law was rarely enforced unless someone brought a charge. A slave's accusation of his master was technically illegal under a Hadrianic rescript that Eusebius preserves; the magistrate first ordered Severus executed for the act of denunciation, and then turned to Apollonius himself.

The case went, unusually, to the Senate. The senators had to decide what to do with one of their own. They gave Apollonius the chance to defend himself; he was a senator, after all, and a philosopher. The Acts of Apollonius — preserved in Greek and Armenian — record the defense. He spoke at length and learnedly on the Christian doctrine of God, the soul, the immortality of the just, the failures of pagan philosophy to attain the truth that Christ had revealed. He treated his accusers with patient courtesy. He admitted to being a Christian. He refused to retract.

The Senate could not acquit him under the existing edicts against the cult. The praetorian prefect, Tigidius Perennis, presided unwillingly. The sentence handed down was beheading — the form of execution due to a Roman senator. He was led out and killed the same day.

His apology, in shorter form, became one of the early Christian texts read aloud in the catechumenate. He had used the only weapon he had — the philosophical learning he had been raised with — and used it without bitterness against men he could have shamed. He demonstrated, in the most public forum the empire offered, that a Christian could also be a gentleman, and that the gospel did not contradict but completed the best of the Greco-Roman mind.

> *"I am a Christian, and for this I give thanks; I cannot deny what I owe my life to."* — Apollonius, before the Senate
