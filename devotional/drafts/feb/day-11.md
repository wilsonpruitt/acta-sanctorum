## February 11

# Calogerus of Ravenna

*Bishop and disciple of Apollinaris, c. 30–c. 130*

He was a Greek by birth — the AASS reads his name *Kalokeros,* "the well-horned one" — and came to Italy as a young man, perhaps with the small Greek-speaking colony that had grown up around the harbor at Ravenna in the late first century. His conversion was the work of Apollinaris, the disciple of Peter sent from Antioch to plant the church at Ravenna in the late forties. The chronicle preserves the small detail: among the converts of Apollinaris's first twelve years, he ordained two priests, *Adhretus and Calocerus*, and made two deacons, *Marcianus the noble and Eleuchadius the philosopher*. Calogerus was perhaps in his early twenties.

He served Apollinaris as one of his vicar-presbyters for the next four decades — through the exile under Vespasian, the second exile under Titus, the eventual return, and the martyrdom under Domitian around the year 79. Three bishops succeeded Apollinaris in turn: Adhretus, then the deacon Eleuchadius, then the deacon Marcianus. Calogerus, by long-standing presence in the see, was the obvious next choice when Marcianus died around 117. The chronicle preserves the medieval detail that the election was confirmed by *a dove descending from heaven on the candidate*, the same form the Ravenna tradition gave to all the early Apollinaris-line elections.

He governed the see through the reign of Hadrian. Hieronymus Rubeus preserves the line: *under Hadrian's tranquil rule, by Calogerus's tireless preaching and God's strengthening of his words, almost all the people of Ravenna abandoned the false gods and embraced the holy religion.* He died, the chronicler adds, *in green old age*, having passed his hundredth year. He was buried at Classe, the harbor-suburb at the mouth of the Po, in the church his successor Probus would later build over his bones. The Saracens destroyed it in the ninth century. The relics were translated to the cathedral in 923, where they have lain since.

The young Greek convert who had heard Apollinaris preach in a tribune's house outlived four of his teacher's successors and finished, at a great age, the conversion of the city the apostles had begun.

> *"Almost all the people of Ravenna abandoned the false gods and embraced the holy religion."* — Hieronymus Rubeus, *Historia Ravennatis* on Calogerus
