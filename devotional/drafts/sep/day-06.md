## September 6

# Magnus of Füssen

*Apostle of the Allgäu, abbot, c. 700–c. 772*

The Vita the Bollandists print under his name is, as they patiently demonstrate over six dense paragraphs, not the original. A monk named Theodorus is supposed to have written it at his bedside; what survives is the ninth-century recension by Ermenric of Ellwangen, smoothed and re-smoothed by later hands, with passages lifted whole from Walafrid Strabo's *Vita Galli.* What can be salvaged is enough: a Frankish-Alemannic monk, raised at Saint Gall under Otmar, sent south into the Allgäu at the request of Bishop Wikterp of Augsburg to evangelize a stretch of upper Bavaria where the Christian gospel had not yet taken hold. He arrived around 740 and stayed for thirty years.

The country he inherited was the Lech valley as it climbs toward the Alps — alpine meadow, dense fir, the small fortified Roman ruin at *Faucibus,* the place we now call Füssen, *the gorges,* where the river squeezes through limestone. He built a cell there, then a wooden chapel, then a stone abbey. The Vita is at its best when it tells the small story: the brewing accident in which the cask miraculously refilled itself; the bear who would not let him fell a tree until he made the sign of the cross; the dragon (or great serpent) of the Roßhaupten gorge who was driven out by the staff he had received from Columbanus's spiritual descendants at Saint Gall. The staff, called the *Magnusstab,* was preserved in the abbey for a thousand years and is still venerated in Füssen today. Local farmers used to bring it to their fields when caterpillars or hail threatened the grain.

He died, according to the Vita, on the sixth of September, an old man among his monks. The abbey at Füssen kept his rule for centuries; the village he founded grew into a market town under the Hohenstaufen castle on the cliff above; his cult spread up and down the Lech and into the Tyrol. *Magnus* — *the Great* — was a name his Latin biographers liked. The Allgäu, more honestly, called him *Mang.* The shorter name is the one his people kept.
