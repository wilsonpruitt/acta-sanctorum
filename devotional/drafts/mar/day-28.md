## March 28

# John of Capistrano

*Franciscan, preacher, 1386–1456*

He was born at Capestrano in the Abruzzi, trained as a lawyer at the University of Perugia, and at twenty-six was made governor of Perugia by King Ladislaus of Naples. Two years later war broke out between Perugia and the Malatesta of Rimini. John, sent to negotiate, was seized and thrown into a Rimini prison cell. He was there for over a year. In the cell, at thirty, he had a vision — Francis of Assisi standing before him in habit — and emerged a different man. He annulled his marriage with his wife's consent (it had never been consummated; both took religious vows) and entered the Franciscan novitiate.

For the next forty years he preached. He preached across Italy, across the Holy Roman Empire, through Bohemia, Poland, Silesia, Hungary; he preached in Latin to the educated and through an interpreter to the rest; he preached for as long as ten hours a day; he preached so often that his voice failed and he kept on with whispered exhortations through his confessors. He reformed Franciscan houses. He served the Council of Florence as a peacemaker between Greeks and Latins. He was the central preacher of the Observant reform of his order.

When he was sixty-nine the Turks were at Belgrade. Constantinople had fallen two years earlier; the Hungarian frontier was the last barrier. Pope Callixtus III sent him north to preach a crusade. John, an old man already, raised a peasant army of perhaps forty thousand and joined János Hunyadi at the river. The siege of Belgrade in July 1456 was the last Christian victory before the long retreat — Belgrade held, Hunyadi died of plague a month later in the same camp, John of plague two months after that. He was seventy.

He had preached to anyone who would listen and led an army when no one else would, and died at the end of both efforts.

> *"He preached till his voice failed; then he prayed till his strength failed."* — the chronicle of his last campaign
