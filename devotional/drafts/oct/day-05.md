## October 5

# Galla of Rome

*Senatorial widow, hidden mystic, c. 489–c. 546*

She was the daughter of Symmachus the consul, the great Roman statesman whom Theodoric the Goth executed in 525 along with her cousin Boethius — Symmachus had stood by his son-in-law to the end and paid for it. Galla was perhaps thirty when her father was killed; she had been recently married, recently widowed, and was suddenly the head of a household stripped of its protector by an Arian king with a long memory. She did not remarry. She gave away the family lands, kept enough to live on, walked across the Tiber to the cluster of small monastic houses that had grown up against the walls of Saint Peter's basilica, and asked to be received as one of them.

Gregory the Great, who had grown up in Rome hearing the Vatican community talk about her, devotes a chapter of his *Dialogues* to her. The story is small and quiet. She had developed, in middle age, a painful condition the physicians of Rome warned her was fatal — they had told her that she would have to remarry to remain healthy, that the celibate life would kill her. She thanked them and stayed. She lived another sixteen years. She slept in a small cell against the wall of the basilica, took the office every night with the brothers across the way, ran a hospice in her front room for the women among the Roman poor whose husbands had died in the Gothic wars and who had nowhere to go. *She was a woman of grave habits,* Gregory writes, *who so much preferred the love of God to the love of the world that she gave her thoughts to nothing else.*

In her last illness she saw, the night before she died, the apostle Peter coming to her cell. She asked him whether her sins were forgiven. *Veni,* he answered — *come.* She asked him to wait three days for her dearest friend, the abbess Benedicta, to be ready to come with her. He nodded; he came back three days later for both of them. Galla died first; Benedicta was buried beside her thirty days later, at the same Mass.

She built no order, wrote no book, founded no monastery, and held a hospice in her front room for sixteen years. The widow of a murdered consul kept a small cell against a basilica wall and lived there until Peter came for her. *She gave her thoughts to nothing else.*

> *"Veni."* — Peter to Galla, in the vision of her last days
