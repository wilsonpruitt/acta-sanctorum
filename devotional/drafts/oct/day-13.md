## October 13

# Theophilus of Antioch

*Sixth bishop of Antioch, apologist of the second century, c. 115–c. 183*

He was born in the country east of the Euphrates, in the small Syriac-speaking villages around Edessa, and came to faith as an adult — *I too,* he writes near the end of the third book *Ad Autolycum,* *was once an unbeliever, until I read the prophets and the gospels and was persuaded by the Holy Spirit.* He was elected sixth bishop of Antioch around 169, succeeding Eros — sixth, that is, in the line that began with Peter — and held the see for fourteen years through the long persecution of Marcus Aurelius and the brief reprieve under Commodus. The Latin tradition remembers him on October 13.

He wrote three books *Ad Autolycum* — *To Autolycus,* a pagan friend who had pressed him for a defense of Christianity against the standard charges of atheism, immorality, and historical novelty — and the books survive intact, perhaps the only complete second-century apology that does. They are slow, patient, often surprising. The first book defends the Christian doctrine of God by the via negativa: *if you say to me, show me your God, I will say to you, show me your man — show me the man behind your eyes, behind the speech that is spoken, behind the desires you cannot see — show him to me, and I will show you my God.* The second runs through the Old Testament chronology against the Greek myths and concludes with the line that gave Christian theology one of its most permanent words. *In a similar way the three days before the lights of heaven were created are figures of the Trinity — God, his Word, and his Wisdom.* It is the first time the word *triad* — *trinitas* — appears in surviving Christian writing.

He wrote also a treatise against Marcion, a refutation of Hermogenes, several catechetical works, and the earliest commentary on the gospels in Greek of which we have notice. None of those survive. What survives is the three books to a friend who had asked an honest question and got, slowly and with great courtesy, an honest answer.

He died at his post around 183, perhaps in his late sixties, and was buried at Antioch in the cathedral he had administered. He was the bishop who first set down what every catechumen since has been taught to confess: that the God who made the world is one God in three. The word came to him not from a council but from a friend's question, and from the patience to answer it three books long.

> *"Show me the man behind your eyes, and I will show you my God."* — Theophilus, *Ad Autolycum* 1.2
