## May 12

# Pancras

*Boy martyr of Rome, d. c. 304*

He was a Phrygian, born at Synnada to noble parents who both died while he was still a child, leaving him to the care of his uncle Dionysius and a deathbed charge to keep the boy as a son. When the family lands called the two of them to Rome, they took a house on the Caelian Hill — and found, hiding in their own street under the persecution, the bishop of Rome himself, Pope Caius. They sought him out, and after twenty days he baptized them both. The faith took in the boy like fire. He and his uncle, the Acts say, offered themselves to the persecutors of their own accord.

Dionysius died first. Then Pancras, fourteen years old, was brought before Diocletian, who was so taken aback by the child in front of him that he tried first to win rather than break him — offering riches, offering to keep him near as a son of his own. The boy was not interested. The emperor turned to threats, and even those Pancras met with a kind of clear contempt: all the terror of your judges and princes, he told Diocletian, is to us no more than a picture we are looking at. The gods you serve are demons, he added, who did not spare even their own parents. It was, the chronicler says, shameful to the emperor to be beaten by a boy. He had him beheaded on the Aurelian Way.

A woman named Octavilla took up the body by night, embalmed it, and laid it in a new tomb, and the Romans did not forget where it was. They said that anyone who swore a false oath at the grave of Pancras was struck down before he could leave it. Three centuries later, when Augustine crossed to England, the first church he raised on the island he gave to this fourteen-year-old — a fitting patron, Gregory thought, for the boys of a new people.

> *Our Lord Jesus Christ has given us such understanding that all the terror of your princes and judges is to us only as much as that painted picture which we see.* — Pancras, before Diocletian
