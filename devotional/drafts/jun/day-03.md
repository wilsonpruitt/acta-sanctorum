## June 3

# Kevin of Glendalough

*Irish abbot, c. 498–618*

He was born of the royal house of Leinster, in the foothills west of Dublin, baptized Coemgen — *the fair-begotten* — by a passing British monk who happened to be in the village. He was sent at seven to be fostered by Petroc the Cumbrian, who had crossed to Ireland for that decade, and at twelve to the monastic school of his uncle Eogain, where he read assiduously among the monks. He was ordained perhaps in his twenties. He fled the offers of a comfortable house outside Dublin, walked alone into the Wicklow mountains, and at the foot of a deep glacial valley called Glendalough — *the glen of two lakes* — found a cave in the cliff above the upper lake and shut himself in it.

He lived there seven years before anyone found him. He slept on stone. He ate cresses and the trout of the lake. He prayed standing in the freezing water of the upper lake at dawn, the chronicler says, with his arms in the form of a cross, until a blackbird settled in his open palm and laid an egg there, and he remained motionless until the egg had hatched and the bird had flown. The story may be a parable. The bare devotion under it is not. Disciples found him in the eighth year, and would not leave; he gave in, came down from the cave, and founded the great monastery of Glendalough on the lower lake. It became one of the four chief monastic schools of Ireland for the next five centuries — a city of round towers, oratories, and bee-hive cells whose ruins still stand in the glen.

He was, the *Vita* says, simple in his diet, plain in his speech, fierce only in his guarding of the silence of the place. He died on the day Bede gives, June 3, 618 — by then a hundred and twenty years old, the chronicler says, though even the Irish chroniclers half-doubt the count. They buried him by the lower lake. The pilgrims still come, on the third of June, around the round tower he never lived to see built.

> *"Why should we not rise at the coming of the servant of God, when the Angels of the Lord arise at his coming in heaven?"* — St. Columba, of Kevin, in the *Life of Coemgen*
