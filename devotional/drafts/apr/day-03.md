## April 3

# Richard of Chichester

*Bishop, 1197–1253*

He was Richard de Wych, born to a modest Worcestershire family that lost its lands during his minority. He and his elder brother, when they came of age, took up the plow themselves and farmed the surviving acres until they could pay off the debts and set the estate right again. Richard, who had wanted to study, gave the restored property to his brother as soon as it could stand on its own. He walked to Oxford at twenty-six with one set of clothes and a borrowed shilling. He stayed twelve years — at Oxford, then Paris, then Bologna for canon law — and came home a learned man.

Edmund of Abingdon, the archbishop of Canterbury, made him his chancellor. When Edmund died in 1240 and was followed by the unpopular Boniface of Savoy, Richard withdrew to a monastery, was ordained priest, and was elected bishop of Chichester in 1244. King Henry III, who had wanted his own candidate, refused to invest him. For two years Richard wandered the diocese on foot, lodging in country priories, doing the work of a bishop without the income of one. The king at last yielded. Richard ate at the same table as his clergy for the rest of his episcopate.

He preached, visited his parishes, defended the rights of the poor against the great landowners of Sussex, and went to bed early. The prayer he is best known for he had written in a quieter form decades earlier and recited daily: *Thanks be to thee, my Lord Jesus Christ, for all the benefits thou hast given me, for all the pains and insults thou hast borne for me. O most merciful Redeemer, friend, and brother, may I know thee more clearly, love thee more dearly, and follow thee more nearly, day by day.*

He died at Dover in 1253, on his way to preach a crusade he was not enthusiastic about, with the prayer on his lips.
