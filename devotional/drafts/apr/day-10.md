## April 10

# Bademus of Persia

*Abbot and martyr, d. 376*

He was a wealthy citizen of Beth-Lapat in Persia who gave up his estate in his middle age, took a few companions into the hills outside the city, and founded a small monastery there. He governed it for some years as abbot. He was perhaps fifty when the Sassanid king Sapor II, in his decades-long persecution of the Persian Christians, had him arrested with seven of his monks.

He was kept in chains for four months. He was beaten daily — the chronicler Sozomen, who had reliable Persian informants, gives the details — and the executioners marvelled that he did not die. At the end of the four months the king's officers produced a young Christian named Nersan, an aristocrat who had apostatized under torture and pledged himself to the king's religion. The officers placed a sword in Nersan's hand and ordered him to kill Bademus. If he refused, they told him, he would be killed himself for backsliding.

Nersan stood with the sword in his hand for some time. He looked at the chained abbot. He raised the sword and could not bring it down. He raised it again. The abbot, looking at him, said only one thing: *strike, my son; the Lord forgives you the blow.* Nersan struck four times before the killing was finished. Bademus prayed for him as he died.

The chronicler completes the story. Nersan walked out of the prison a free man, into the kingdom whose religion he had embraced. He went mad within months. He could not stop seeing the abbot's face. He drew his own sword on himself, the same sword, and ended his own life.

Bademus had taken upon himself the death of the executor as well as his own. The mercy he had asked for the boy with the sword was the mercy he could not, in the end, give him from this side of the grave.
