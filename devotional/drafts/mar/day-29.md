## March 29

# Berthold of Carmel

*Founder of the Carmelites, d. c. 1195*

He was the son of a French noble house — kin, the chronicles say, of Aymeric of Limoges, who became Latin Patriarch of Antioch — and he came east on the Third Crusade. He fought in the field. He was at the siege of Antioch when the army contracted dysentery and the besiegers became the besieged, and he was nearly dead of fever in his tent when, in a vision, Christ reproached him for fighting on the wrong battleground. Berthold rose. He took off his armor. He walked out of the army and south.

He climbed Mount Carmel — the long ridge above the bay of Acre that the prophet Elijah had once climbed to confront the priests of Baal. There were already hermits living in caves along its western flank, some of them descended in spirit (and some, they insisted, in flesh) from the prophets. They lived alone. Berthold proposed to them that they live also together. He built an oratory dedicated to the Mother of God between their cells; he drew up a brief rule of life; he became their first prior. The community, by the time he died, had a name: the Brothers of the Blessed Virgin Mary of Mount Carmel.

What followed him was larger than he could have imagined. Within fifty years Albert of Jerusalem would give the community its formal Rule. Within a century the Carmelites would be a great mendicant order across Europe. Within four centuries Teresa of Ávila and John of the Cross would reform them, and write the books of Christian mysticism that have not been matched.

Berthold himself appears in the historical record only briefly — the Carmelite tradition holds him to be its founder, the modern historians regard the founding as gradual — but every Carmelite altar still names him in its founding prayer.

> *"Set your fire on this mountain, as on the other."* — Berthold's prayer at Carmel
