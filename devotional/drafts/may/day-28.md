## May 28

# Germanus of Paris

*Merovingian bishop and almoner, c. 496–576*

He was born near Autun in Burgundy, of decent family, and so nearly killed in the womb — his mother, the *Vita* by Fortunatus says, attempted twice to miscarry him for shame at the close spacing of her pregnancies — that the chronicler returns to the detail as the first sign of God's hand on him. He was raised by a cousin who was a priest, ordained young, and made abbot of Saint-Symphorien at Autun by his fortieth year. The house under him became famous for almsgiving: he gave away grain so freely that the brothers once locked the storeroom against him, and the saint, the chronicler says, found the key by sleeping outside the door until it was returned.

Childebert I, the Frankish king of Paris, having visited Autun and recognized the abbot, recalled him to the capital in 555 and pressed him into the vacant see of Paris. Germanus accepted on the condition that the king found an abbey for the poor outside the city walls — the house that would become Saint-Germain-des-Prés. He served as bishop for twenty-one years. He spent the revenues of his see almost entirely on captives and the poor; he is said to have ransomed so many slaves from Spanish merchants on the Seine that the merchants began to avoid the Paris market. He was a constant petitioner before the four Merovingian kings who sat on the Paris throne in his lifetime, restraining (where he could) the fratricidal wars of the dynasty and excommunicating Charibert I after the king's incestuous remarriage.

He died on May 28, 576, in his eightieth year. The chronicler notes the small thing: that the kitchen, on the morning of his death, ran out of grain for the daily distribution at the gate, and the bishop ordered the granary opened entire, and there was bread that day for everyone, and none for the bishop's table. Paris buried him in the basilica he had founded for the poor; it bears his name.

> *With this bishop the voice of the poor man weighed more than the voice of the king.* — from his Life
