## June 7

# Robert of Newminster

*Cistercian abbot of Northumberland, c. 1100–1159*

He was a Yorkshire parish priest, of a settled benefice and a settled life, when the founding of the Cistercian abbey of Fountains in 1132 unsettled him. The founders were a small band of Benedictines who had walked out of Saint Mary's at York demanding a stricter rule, and had spent their first winter in the open under a great elm by the river Skell, eating boiled elm-leaves. Robert read of them in his rectory and went north to join them. He was thirty-two. He gave up his benefice and slept the rest of his life on straw.

He was at Fountains seven years — through the bitterest of the founding privations, when the brothers ate the bread of three days in five and twice nearly disbanded — and was singled out by the abbot for his patience under the discipline. In 1137 the baron Ralph de Merlay offered the order land at Morpeth in Northumberland for a daughter house, and Robert was sent to found it with eleven monks. They called it Newminster. He governed it for the rest of his life.

He was a man of quiet temperament whom the chronicler remembers chiefly for two things. He kept his fast without relief: he never rose satisfied from the table, in Lent ate only bread and water, and chanted the whole Psalter — a hundred and fifty psalms — to the Lord each day beyond the appointed office. And he kept one friendship above all others, walking down to Finchale on the Wear to sit with Godric the Hermit, to whom he made the confession of his conscience; the two would talk, the chronicler says, day with nights, of the diseases of the vices and the remedies of the virtues.

He died at Newminster on the night of June 7, 1159, having celebrated the chapter Mass that morning. The abbey was suppressed in 1537. The thirteen mossed stones of the cloister are still visible in the field above the river Wansbeck.

> *"If I had oatmeal bread smeared with butter, I think I could eat from it."* — Robert, when fasting had left him unable to eat what was set before him
