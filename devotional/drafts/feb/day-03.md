## February 3

# Ansgar of Hamburg-Bremen

*Apostle of the North, 801–865*

He was a Frankish boy from Picardy, raised at the abbey of Corbie under the same Adelard who had refused Charlemagne. He learned what Corbie taught: Latin, the Rule, missionary instinct. When he was twenty-five, the Danish king Harald Klak, who had just received baptism in the Carolingian court, asked for a chaplain to take home with him to the Danes. Ansgar volunteered. He spent the next forty years trying to plant the Church among peoples who were sailing south every summer to burn the churches he had grown up in.

He made two long missions to Sweden. On the first, in 829, he reached the trading port of Birka and built a small church — the first Christian church in Scandinavia. The community lasted; the king Olof gave grudging permission for the mission to continue; Ansgar baptized merchants and slaves and a few of the noble families. On the second, in his fifties, he came back to find the church still there but the converts struggling and his missionary clergy thin. He gave them another decade. He was made archbishop of Hamburg, then (after the Vikings burned Hamburg in 845) of Hamburg-Bremen jointly; the pope made him papal legate for the whole of Scandinavia. He never had political backing strong enough to protect the work.

He did not see Scandinavia converted in his lifetime. The mission collapsed for half a century after his death and was resumed only under his successors. What he had built — the church at Birka, the see at Hamburg-Bremen, the precedent of a Frankish bishop going north — would eventually carry the Faith into Sweden and Norway, but only at a hundred years' remove.

*If only the Lord would grant me one miracle*, he said in old age — *that he might make me a better man.* It is the only miracle he prayed for, and the only one he wanted credited to his account.
