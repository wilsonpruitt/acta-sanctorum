## May 11

# Mamertus of Vienne

*Bishop, d. c. 475*

He was a Gallo-Roman bishop in the city of Vienne, on the Rhône south of Lyon, in the years when the Western Empire was visibly disintegrating. The Burgundians had moved into the upper Rhône valley; Vienne was on the southern edge of their kingdom. The fields around Vienne in the 460s were beset by a chain of calamities: late frosts that killed the grain, wildfires in the surrounding forests, an earthquake that brought down part of the basilica, wolves coming into the streets at night from the abandoned villas of the countryside. The Christian community in the city was frightened. The pagan remnant was angry.

On Easter Eve of one of those years, a fire broke out near the basilica during the vigil. Mamertus, vesting in the sacristy, came out to find his city on the edge of panic. He led the people in prayer through the night and the fire turned. The next morning he announced that the Christian community would, beginning that week, hold a three-day public procession of penance — a great litany, sung antiphonally, through the streets and the fields outside the walls — to ask God for mercy on Vienne. The procession was to be held on the three days before the Ascension and would be repeated every year. The clergy walked; the people walked; the sick were carried; the great litany of supplication was chanted at every shrine.

The calamities eased that year. The practice took. Within a generation other Gallic dioceses had adopted it. Within a century the Roman liturgy itself had received it as the *Rogation Days* — the *rogationes*, the askings — to be observed across the Latin Church on the same days each year. It was, perhaps, the only liturgical institution of a Gallo-Roman bishop to become a universal observance of the Western Church.

He had asked his city to ask. They asked. The asking has gone on for fifteen hundred years.

> *"Let knowledge ask, let love seek, let labor knock."* — Avitus of Vienne, homily on the Rogations of Mamertus
