## June 8

# Medard of Noyon

*Frankish bishop, c. 456–545*

He was born at Salency near Noyon in Picardy, of a Frankish father and a Gallo-Roman mother, in the years when the western empire had just dissolved and the Salian Franks were settling permanently among the people they had conquered. His parents, the chronicler says, were equally Christian and equally well-off, and he was the first child of either kindred to be brought up entirely within the faith. He was, by every account, a tender child. The biographer keeps two stories from his boyhood. He gave away one of his father's horses — entrusted to his keeping — to a poor traveler whose own horse had died, and when the count came up short the missing horse was found miraculously restored; his father, on hearing the reason, embraced him rather than reproach him. Caught as a boy in a sudden storm, the chronicler says, he was sheltered from the downpour by a great eagle that hovered over him, holding off the rain.

He was made a priest at thirty-three and bishop of Vermandois — the see of Saint-Quentin, later transferred to Noyon — at fifty-three. He governed fifteen years. He outlived three Frankish kings, baptized the fourth (Lothar I), and helped re-found the eastern see of Tournai when the Gallo-Roman bishops had fled it. He founded the abbey of Soissons, and was remembered through his episcopate as patient under trouble and open-handed to the wretched.

He died, the chronicler says, on a wet June morning at Noyon in 545. The peasants of Picardy say it rains on his feast for forty days, because Medard always weeps a little when he sees the spring crops short.

> *He was patient in tribulation, succouring the wretched with large pity.* — from the ancient Life of Medard
