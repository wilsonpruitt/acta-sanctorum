## July 12

# John Gualbert

*Founder of the Vallombrosans, c. 985–1073*

He was the eldest son of a Florentine knight named Gualbertus, of the small *cattani* nobility of the contado, raised in the usual way of his class to ride, fight, and revenge. His younger brother Hugh was murdered, probably about 1010, in a street feud with another Florentine knight named Anselm. John spent the next several years looking for Anselm. He found him at last, alone and on horseback, on a narrow road outside Florence on a Good Friday afternoon. He drew his sword. Anselm, who had no escape, dismounted and knelt in the road. He spread his arms, the chronicler says, in the form of a cross, and asked John in the name of the day to spare him.

John sat his horse a long moment. He sheathed the sword, dismounted, embraced Anselm in the dust of the road, and rode on to the abbey of San Miniato al Monte on the hill above Florence. He tied his horse at the gate, walked in alone, and prostrated himself before the great Romanesque crucifix that still hangs there. The crucifix, the chronicler says, inclined its head to him in answer. He never went home. He cut off his own long noble's hair with a knife at the steps of the altar, asked for the habit, and was received as a novice.

He spent seven years at San Miniato. He left when the abbot was succeeded by a simoniac whose appointment had been bought, and he refused to accept the new abbot's rule. He went north with two companions, into the wooded valley of *Vallis Umbrosa* — the shadowed valley — in the Apennines above Reggello, and founded a hermitage there about 1036. Disciples came. The Vallombrosan order grew, with him as its first abbot, into a dozen houses across Tuscany in his lifetime. He kept the Benedictine rule but pushed it toward stricter observance: longer night office, more silence, no meat, no eggs.

His public work was the war against simony — the buying and selling of church offices that the Gregorian reform was just beginning to confront. He preached against it through northern Italy for four decades. He encouraged his monks, in 1063, to put the Florentine bishop Peter Mezzabarba to the *ordeal of fire*: the monk Peter Igneus walked, the chronicler says, unharmed through a fire to demonstrate that the bishop's office had been bought. The bishop was deposed. John died at his abbey of San Salvi outside Florence, July 12, 1073.

> *"I forgave him in the road; I will forgive no buyer of the church in the cathedral."* — John, on simony
