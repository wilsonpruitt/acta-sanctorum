## January 6

# The Epiphany

*The manifestation of Christ to the Gentiles*

They came from beyond the Roman world, scholars of stars whose names the gospel does not bother to record. Tradition would later call them Caspar and Melchior and Balthasar and number them at three; the gospel only says *wise men from the East*, and *they brought gifts*. They had read a sign in the night sky and walked, by the only road they knew how to take, in the direction it pointed. The road led them to Jerusalem. They assumed, reasonably, that a new king would be born in a palace, and so they went to a palace and asked.

Herod heard them out and called for his priests, who knew the prophets. *Bethlehem*, the priests said — almost without thinking. Herod sent the foreigners south with a courteous lie about wanting to honor the child himself. The wise men went on. The star, which they had not seen since Jerusalem, returned to them on the road, and led them to a house that was no palace at all. They had walked for months. They knelt before a toddler.

They opened their treasures and laid out gold for a king, frankincense for a priest, and myrrh for the burial of a man — not yet knowing why. A warning came that night in a dream: do not return to Herod. They left Bethlehem by a different road and disappeared from the gospel forever.

The Epiphany is the showing-forth of Christ. The early Church understood it just as much as the showing-forth of *who he came for*. The first to find him after the shepherds were neither rabbis nor soldiers nor priests, but foreigners following a sign no Jew had been given. May our roads, however oddly they turn, still bring us to the same house.

> *"When they saw the star, they rejoiced with exceeding great joy."* — Matthew 2:10
