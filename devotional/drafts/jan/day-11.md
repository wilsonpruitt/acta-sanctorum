## January 11

# Theodosius the Cenobiarch

*Abbot, father of Palestinian cenobitism, 423–529*

He was Cappadocian by birth, a near-contemporary of Sabbas the Sanctified, and he came to the Judean desert when he was about thirty, planning to live as a hermit. The hermits' caves along the road from Jerusalem to Bethlehem were filling up; men came to him for counsel and would not leave. He moved deeper into the wilderness, and they followed. At last, persuaded that solitude alone was not his calling, he built a great communal monastery on a hill east of Bethlehem and made the gathering official.

What he founded was unlike any monastery the East had yet seen. He laid out four churches in the same compound — one for each of the languages his monks spoke: Greek, Armenian, Bessian, and Arabic — and the offices were prayed in all four. He built a hospital for the sick. He built a hospice for the old. He set apart a separate quarter for the mentally ill and another for the homeless poor. By the end of his life four hundred men lived under his rule, and the patriarchs of Jerusalem confirmed him as *cenobiarch*, the supervisor of all communal monasteries in Palestine.

In his old age the emperor Anastasius sent him a substantial bribe to support the Monophysite party against Chalcedon. Theodosius gave the money to the poor and wrote back that he and his monks would not betray the faith of the apostles for any throne. He was briefly exiled. He returned. He lived to one hundred and five.

He had taken a desert that had been the refuge of solitaries and turned it into a country of hospitable houses. What he taught his sons was that the silence of God is best kept not alone but in company.

> *"We will defend the faith of the apostles."* — Theodosius, in answer to the emperor
