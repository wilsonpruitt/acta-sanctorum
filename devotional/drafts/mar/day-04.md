## March 4

# Casimir of Poland

*Prince, 1458–1484*

He was the second son of King Casimir IV of Poland and a careful, melancholy young man from his early youth. The court tutors found him an apt pupil; the king's marshals found him an unwilling soldier; the courtiers found him a poor courtier and a strange friend. He preferred the cathedral to the council chamber. He slept on the floor beside his bed when no one was watching. He gave away the contents of his purse so often that the chamberlain stopped replenishing it.

His father sent him at fifteen to invade Hungary at the head of an army of twelve thousand men: the Polish-Lithuanian succession claim against Matthias Corvinus, the Hungarian king, had become a question of arms. Casimir, on the road, came to the judgment that the war was unjust and that the dispute should be settled at the negotiating table. He halted the army at the frontier and refused to cross. His father was furious and banished him to a country castle for a year. Casimir went without protest.

He was twenty-five when consumption took him. The chronicles say that as the illness deepened he asked daily for the singing of the Latin hymn *Omni die dic Mariae*, the daily hymn to the Virgin, which some traditions credit to him as composer; he sang it himself until he could no longer sing. He died at dawn on March 4, 1484, at Hrodna in Lithuania. They buried him at Vilnius, where his tomb has been the central pilgrimage shrine of the Lithuanian Catholic Church ever since.

He had been asked, once, why he never married. *I will marry one*, he had told his confessor, *who will not be widowed when I die.*
