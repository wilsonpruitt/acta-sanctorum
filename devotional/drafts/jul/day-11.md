## July 11

# Pius I, Pope

*Roman pontiff of the second century, d. c. 155*

He was the brother of Hermas — the same Hermas who wrote the *Shepherd*, the long allegorical work that nearly entered the New Testament canon and was read aloud in many churches for the next three centuries as if it had. The *Muratorian Fragment*, the oldest surviving list of accepted Christian writings, gives the relation plainly: the *Shepherd* was recently written in our times in the city of Rome by Hermas, while his brother Pius held the chair of the Roman church. The two brothers, the same fragment says, had been slaves of a Christian household and had been freed.

He governed the Roman church for perhaps fifteen years, from about 140 to 155, in the long peace of the reigns of Antoninus Pius and the early years of Marcus Aurelius. He had to deal with the heretic Marcion, who arrived in Rome from Sinope in Pontus during his pontificate, made a substantial donation to the Roman church, taught his pruned gospel of an unknown alien God for several years in the city, and was at last excommunicated by Pius in 144. The donation was returned. He had also to deal with the gnostic Valentinus, then teaching in Rome and gathering a following that would not formally break with the church for another fifty years. He kept them just within the bounds in his lifetime.

Of his own writing little survives. A single pastoral letter, on the keeping of Easter, addressed to the bishop of Vienne in Gaul, is preserved in the *Liber Pontificalis*. A baptismal formula, perhaps composed under him, may underlie the Apostles' Creed in its earliest Roman form. He founded, the chronicler says, two of the *tituli* of the city: *titulus Pudentis* on the Esquiline (later Santa Pudenziana) and *titulus Praxedis* (later Santa Prassede), both on land donated by Christian families — the Pudens family whose name Paul mentions in second Timothy, and the family of Praxedes (July 21 in this book).

He died, the tradition says, a martyr, about 155, though the manner is not preserved. He had governed the Roman church through the years its earliest creeds and its earliest property-bases took shape. He was the brother who had stayed at the chair while Hermas had written.

> *"What we have received, the same we hand on; nothing of our own; nothing of any man's; only of the Lord."* — Pius, in his letter to Vienne
