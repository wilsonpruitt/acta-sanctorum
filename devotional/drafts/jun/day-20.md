## June 20

# Silverius, Pope

*Roman pontiff and martyr, d. 539*

He was the legitimate son, born before his father's ordination, of Pope Hormisdas (514–523), and was raised at the Lateran in the years his father was reconciling the Latin and Greek churches after the long Acacian schism. He took orders, served as a subdeacon, then a deacon. He was a quiet man, of moderate parts, of his father's blood and his father's instincts: hold what has been received. He was forty-something when the Ostrogothic king Theodahad — at war with the emperor Justinian and the general Belisarius then advancing through Italy — looked for a Roman to elevate to the chair of Peter who would be loyal to Gothic Italy. He picked Silverius and pressed the Roman clergy hard. They consecrated him under protest in June 536.

Belisarius took Rome by negotiation six months later. The empress Theodora at Constantinople, who had her own plans for the Roman see, sent letters to Belisarius asking him to depose Silverius and replace him with the deacon Vigilius, then in the imperial capital, who had privately promised to overturn the Council of Chalcedon and rehabilitate the Monophysite Anthimus, Theodora's protégé. Silverius refused on every point. He would not condemn Chalcedon. He would not seat Anthimus. He knew, the chronicler says, what the refusal would cost him.

He was summoned to Belisarius's headquarters in the Pincian palace on March 20, 537. The general's wife Antonina received him on a couch, with Belisarius at her feet. She read out forged letters purporting to show Silverius's correspondence with the Goths against the empire. He was stripped of his vestments on the spot, dressed in a monk's tunic, and exiled the next day to Patara, a city of Lycia on the south Anatolian coast. Vigilius was consecrated pope in his place. The bishop of Patara, on receiving the exile, went to Constantinople himself to protest; Justinian, embarrassed, ordered the trial reopened. Silverius was returned to Italy and the matter remitted for investigation. He never reached Rome. Antonina intercepted him on the road, had him deported to the small malarial island of Palmarola off Naples, and left him there. He died of starvation on the island, June 20, 539.

> *"Lady Augusta, I will never recall a heretical man, condemned in his wickedness."* — Silverius, to the empress's envoy
