## February 9

# Cyril of Alexandria

*Patriarch, doctor, c. 376–444*

He inherited the see of Alexandria from his uncle Theophilus in 412 — at a moment when the city was the most theologically combative on earth — and governed it for thirty-two years. He was a difficult man. He could be authoritarian; he was involved in some of the ugliest civic violence of his day; his Christological vocabulary was sharp enough that even his admirers spent the next century trying to soften it. He is also one of the half-dozen most important theologians the Church has produced.

The fight that defined him was with Nestorius, patriarch of Constantinople, over how to speak of Mary. Nestorius preferred to call her Mother of Christ rather than Mother of God; the distinction, he said, protected the Lord's full divinity from being entangled with a human birth. Cyril answered that the distinction *separated* what God had joined. *We do not say that the Word became flesh by changing into flesh*, he wrote in his second letter to Nestorius — *the change is in our nature, not his.* If Mary was not Mother of God, the child she bore was not God; the salvation of the human race had not happened in her womb. The Council of Ephesus in 431 ruled for Cyril.

He spent the next decade trying to make peace with the Antiochene theologians who shared most of his beliefs but few of his words. The Formula of Reunion in 433 was his work. It cost him politically; he had to retract some of the harshest phrases he had used. He did not enjoy retracting; he did it anyway, because the Church mattered to him more than the victory.

The Council of Chalcedon, twenty years after his death, would build on his Christology and correct his excesses. He had given the next generation both the language and the lesson — that even when you are right, you may have spoken too sharply, and the truth will outlast your sharpness only if you can be made gentler.

> *"We do not say that the Word became flesh by changing into flesh; the change is in our nature, not his."* — Cyril, *Second Letter to Nestorius*
