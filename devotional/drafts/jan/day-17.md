## January 17

# Anthony the Great

*Abbot, father of monks, c. 251–356*

He was eighteen, newly orphaned, when he walked into church and heard the Gospel read: *If you would be perfect, go, sell what you have and give to the poor.* He walked out and did it. The land his parents had left him — three hundred fertile acres along the Nile — he gave to his neighbors. The household goods he sold. The proceeds he distributed, keeping only a small sum for his younger sister. A week later he heard another verse: *Take no thought for tomorrow.* He gave that sum away too, placed his sister in the care of consecrated virgins, and went out to the edge of his village to pray.

The desert, for Anthony, was not yet a place. It was an intention. He learned prayer from one old hermit, fasting from another, vigilance from a third, gathering virtues like a bee, as his biographer says, from every flower. Only when he had learned them did he walk beyond the inhabited world, cross the Nile, and shut himself in an abandoned fortress. For twenty years he spoke to no one. Bread and water were passed through a small window twice a year.

When at last he came out, people expected a wasted man. Instead, the biographer writes, "his body kept its former state, neither grown fat from idleness nor wasted from fasting and wrestling with demons; but such as they had known him before his retirement." He had been fighting, and he had been strengthened. To the friends who crowded around him he said only this: *I saw the snares of the enemy spread over the whole earth, and I groaned and said, Who can escape them? And I heard a voice answering: Humility.*
