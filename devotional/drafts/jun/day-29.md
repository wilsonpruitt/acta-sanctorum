## June 29

# Peter and Paul

*The two apostles of Rome, d. c. 64–67*

They had quarreled in Antioch. Paul writes about it himself in the letter to the Galatians, plainly and without softening, perhaps fifteen years before either of them died. Peter had come down from Jerusalem, had eaten with the Gentile converts in the manner Paul had taught — until the men from James arrived, and Peter, the senior apostle, drew back from the Gentile table. Paul withstood him to the face. He says so. The collision was sharp. They went on. They had agreed in the end on the substance of the gospel; they had divided the territories — Peter to the circumcision, Paul to the uncircumcision — they had given each other the right hand of fellowship and not gone home enemies.

They came at last to the same city. Peter to Rome by the long way around, through Pontus and the diaspora cities, perhaps by Corinth, arriving in the capital of the empire he had been told once at Caesarea Philippi he would govern in another sense. Paul to Rome under arrest, brought from Caesarea Maritima after appealing his case to Caesar, in chains, by sea, in the winter of 60-61. They lived in the city together for what seems to have been some years before the persecution. Tradition is firm: Peter governed the church of Rome from the gentile community he had inherited from his predecessors; Paul preached from the rented house Acts ends with, the one where he received all who came. They were in Rome together when the city burned in July of 64.

Nero blamed the Christians. The persecution that followed lasted through Nero's last four years. Peter, by the ancient tradition first attested in Tertullian, was crucified at Rome — head downward, at his own request, because he would not be killed in the manner of his Lord — at the foot of the Vatican hill, in or near the circus of Caligula and Nero. They buried him on the slope above. Paul, a Roman citizen, was not crucified but beheaded — by tradition, at the third milestone on the Ostian Way, at a place called Aquae Salviae. The two basilicas at Saint Peter's and Saint Paul Outside the Walls stand over the two graves.

The church has kept their feast on one day from very early. The argument in Antioch was real; the unity in Rome was real; the church has needed both.

> *"He withstood him to the face, because he was to be blamed."* — Paul of Peter, *Galatians* 2:11
