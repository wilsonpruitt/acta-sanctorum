## March 18

# Cyril of Jerusalem

*Bishop, doctor, c. 315–386*

He was born in the city he would later govern, in the years just before Constantine's mother built the church of the Holy Sepulchre over what the Christians believed was the empty tomb. Cyril grew up watching pilgrims arrive at the new shrine, learning to read in Greek and Aramaic both, and entered the clergy of Jerusalem in his twenties. He was made bishop around 350.

His great work was teaching. He preached to the catechumens — the adult candidates for baptism — in the great church his predecessors had built, and his lectures were taken down in shorthand by those listening. The twenty-three lectures that survive, the *Catechetical Lectures*, are the most complete record we have of how the fourth-century Church taught its converts: the creed line by line, the meaning of baptism, the structure of the Mass, the discipline of the Christian life. They are the catechism of Jerusalem at the moment when Jerusalem first had pilgrims again.

His episcopate was not quiet. The Arian controversy was raging; Cyril, who held the Nicene faith but was suspicious of certain Nicene phrasings, was deposed and exiled three times — by Acacius of Caesarea, by Constantius, by Valens — and reinstated three times. He lost sixteen of his thirty-six years as bishop to exile. He attended the Council of Constantinople in 381, the council that confirmed Nicaea and added the clauses on the Holy Spirit, and signed its decrees with what must have been a tired hand.

He died at Jerusalem at seventy. His lectures had gone everywhere by then; Jerome cited them, Egeria the pilgrim cited them, the next century's catechumens were still being taught with his book in hand.

> *"You have been called a Christian. Be aware of the name you bear."* — Cyril, *Catechetical Lectures* I
