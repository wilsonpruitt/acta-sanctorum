## May 9

# Pachomius the Great

*Founder of cenobitic monasticism, c. 292–348*

He was a young Egyptian conscript in the Roman army of Constantine, taken from his village in the upper Nile in his twenties and marched downriver in chains with the other recruits. He was not a Christian. The army stopped one night at a small town called Esna, where the local Christians came out at evening with food and water for the prisoners — not knowing who they were, not asking what gods they served, only knowing that men in chains needed feeding. Pachomius, who had grown up in a religion that did not feed strangers, watched the bread come into his hand and asked the soldier next to him what the people were doing.

When the war ended and he was discharged, he kept the question. He sought out a Christian community, was baptized, attached himself to a hermit named Palamon, and learned the desert disciplines for seven years. He was thirty. He was praying alone at a place called Tabennesi on a bend of the Nile when, the chronicler says, a voice told him to stay there and build a community. He had not thought of community. The desert tradition he had inherited from Anthony was solitary. He stayed anyway.

What he founded was new. Pachomius proposed that monks live together under a written Rule — fixed hours of prayer, common meals, common work, a hierarchy of superiors. The hermits at Tabennesi were skeptical. His brother John, who came down to investigate, became his first disciple. Other men followed. By Pachomius's death, twenty-eight years later, the *koinonia* of Tabennesi was nine monasteries housing perhaps three thousand monks, with his sister Mary running an affiliated women's community across the river.

Athanasius came to visit him once. Cassian would later carry his Rule to the West. Benedict would build on Cassian. Every Christian community on earth that has ever lived under a written common Rule traces, ultimately, to the moment Pachomius decided to stay at Tabennesi.

> *"Stay here and build."* — the voice Pachomius heard at Tabennesi
