## June 4

# Petroc of Cornwall

*Welsh prince, abbot of Bodmin, sixth century*

He was a younger son of a Welsh king of Glamorgan, sent in his teens to the great monastic school of Llancarfan, and afterward — in the way of his generation — to Ireland, where the schools were finer still. He spent twenty years there. He came back to the western tip of Britain a middle-aged abbot, landed in north Cornwall at the mouth of the Camel river, and founded the monastery of Lanwethinoc on the slope above the harbor — the place now called Padstow, *Petroc's-stow*, his town. He governed it thirty years. He sent missions inland across the Bodmin moors and founded, perhaps near the end of his life, the daughter house at Bodmin itself, which would outlive Padstow as the chief seat of his cult.

The Cornish chronicler keeps three stories. Petroc was once preaching on a beach when a stag, half-spent, ran out of the trees and lay at his feet; behind it came a king's hunting party with hounds. Petroc spread his cloak over the stag and would not move. The king, the chronicler says, dismounted and asked the abbot's blessing instead. Petroc found a wolf the Lord had appointed to guard his staff and sheepskin, and the beast became his companion and would not leave his side. And Petroc once, near the end of his life, walked away from Padstow without telling anyone, took ship for the East, and was gone seven years — pilgrim through Jerusalem, hermit for a time, the *Vita* says, on a small island in the Indian sea — before returning to find his monastery wondering whether he was dead. He took up the abbacy again and ruled it three more years.

He died on a pastoral visit to the daughter house at Bodmin, June 4, perhaps 564, perhaps later. Padstow held his bones until the twelfth century; Bodmin holds them now, in an ivory casket that survived the Reformation buried in a parish wall. He had been a king's son who would not be king. He had been an abbot who could not stop walking.

> *Repress fury, flee lies, abhor envy; suspect no evil of a neighbor; and prepare the heart as a pure dwelling for God.* — from his last instruction to his disciples
