## April 11

# Stanislaus of Krakow

*Bishop and martyr, 1030–1079*

He was born at Szczepanów in southern Poland to noble parents who had waited thirty years for a child and had vowed to give the boy to God if one was granted. They kept the vow. He was educated at Gniezno and at Liège in the Low Countries, came home a priest, and was made canon and then preacher at the cathedral of Krakow. He was elected bishop of Krakow in 1072. He was forty-two.

The king of Poland in those years was Boleslaus II, called *the Bold*, a brilliant and reckless ruler whose conquests had won the kingdom new territory and whose private conduct was a public scandal. He had kidnapped the wife of a nobleman named Mściszko and was keeping her as his concubine. He had levied unjust taxes. He had executed magnates for disagreement. Stanislaus, the new bishop, warned him privately first, then publicly, then formally — and at last, when no warning had effect, declared the king excommunicate.

Boleslaus rode to the small church of Saint Michael on the rock outside Krakow on April 11, 1079. Stanislaus was celebrating Mass at the altar. The king walked in with his guards and ordered them to seize the bishop. They refused. The king drew his own sword and killed Stanislaus where he stood, at the altar, in the middle of the canon. The chronicle of Gallus Anonymus records the king's later remorse — Boleslaus fled Poland the same year, ended his days in obscurity in Hungary — and Gregory VII placed Poland under interdict.

Stanislaus has been the patron of Poland since his canonization in 1253. He stood, against a king, on the simplest principle a bishop can hold: that no power on earth can sanctify what God has forbidden.

> *"You shall not put away the wife of your neighbor."* — Stanislaus to the king
