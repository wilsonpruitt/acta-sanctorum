## April 6

# Methodius

*Apostle to the Slavs, c. 815–885*

He was the elder brother of Cyril, more reserved in temperament and slower to read a room — a Byzantine civil official before he was a monk, governor of a Slavic-speaking border province in his thirties. He resigned the office in his middle years and entered a monastery on Mount Olympus in Bithynia. He was the steady one. Cyril was the brilliant one. The mission they took together to Moravia in 862 belonged equally to both, but the man who held the work together for sixteen years after Cyril's death in Rome was Methodius.

He was consecrated archbishop of Sirmium — the old Roman see for Pannonia, lapsed for centuries — and sent back to Moravia by Pope Hadrian II to govern the new Slavic Church. He was forty-five then. He found the Frankish bishops of Bavaria furious at having lost ecclesiastical jurisdiction over the region. They arrested him on his journey north and imprisoned him for two and a half years in Swabia. Pope John VIII secured his release. He went on to Moravia, finished the translation of the Old Testament into Slavonic that he and Cyril had begun, established a Slavonic clergy, and was twice more dragged to Rome to defend his work against the Franks. He won both times.

He died at Velehrad on April 6, 885, surrounded by his disciples, after celebrating the Slavonic liturgy one last time on Palm Sunday. His final blessing went to the priest Gorazd, whom he named as his successor; within months of his death the Slavonic mission in Moravia was suppressed by the local prince at Frankish pressure, and the disciples were driven out. They went south. They took the alphabet, the books, and the liturgy with them into Bulgaria, where the work would take root and from which it would later spread to Russia.

He had outlived the brother he loved most and the work he could see immediately around him. What he had built endured by way of refugees.

> *"Bury me where I have lived. Christ goes with the body whether it is buried in honor or in exile."* — Methodius, on his deathbed
