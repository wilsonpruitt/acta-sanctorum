## October 2

# Leodegar of Autun

*Bishop blinded in the politics of the Merovingian collapse, c. 616–679*

He was a Frankish nobleman of the Burgundian aristocracy, raised at the court of the dowager queen Bathild who had been his guardian after his father's early death, educated at Poitiers under his uncle the bishop, ordained deacon, then archdeacon, then in 663 sent up the Saône to Autun as bishop of one of the most important sees in Burgundy. He stayed there for nine years and rebuilt the city. Autun in the 660s had been gutted by the dynastic fighting around the Merovingian throne; the cathedral was unroofed, the canonical residence was a ruin, the diocese had not had a settled bishop for three years. Leodegar repaired the buildings, restored the schools, fed the poor through three bad harvests, and held the western synod of Autun in 670 that issued the canons against the Monothelite heresy and the Trinitarian formulas the Western church would adopt for a thousand years.

Then Frankish politics caught up with him. Ebroin, the mayor of the palace under the boy-king Theuderic III, had been deposed in 673 in favor of a more moderate party; in 675 he came back, and he came back hunting the men who had deposed him. Leodegar had been one of them. The bishop was arrested at Autun in 676, brought to Ebroin's villa at Fécamp, blinded with a hot iron, his lips and tongue cut out, and held for two years in a Norman monastery to make sure he died slowly. He did not die. In 678 Ebroin had him pulled from the cell, given a hasty trial in absentia, deposed from his see, and beheaded in the forest of Sarcing in Artois.

His brother Gerin, who had stood by him through the whole captivity and refused to renounce him, was stoned to death in 676 at the same king's villa, in the gory parallel the Bollandists devote a long opening section to documenting. Their joint *acta* — the older Vita by an anonymous Autun cleric who knew Leodegar personally, then the longer recension by a monk of Saint-Symphorien — survive together. The translation of Leodegar's body to the abbey of Saint-Maixent in Poitou in 681 set off a cult that ran across half of France: more than two hundred medieval parishes were dedicated to him, and the village of Saint-Léger turns up in nearly every department.

He held his see for nine years and was tortured for three, and the tortured years are the ones the church remembered.

> *"Etiam si me totum lacerare voluerit, quod meum est non perdam."* — Leodegar, to Ebroin's interrogator
