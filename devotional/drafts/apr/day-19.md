## April 19

# Alphege of Canterbury

*Archbishop, martyr, 954–1012*

He was a West Country Englishman, monk at Deerhurst as a young man and prior at Glastonbury after that, abbot of Bath, bishop of Winchester at the unusually young age of thirty. He was made archbishop of Canterbury in 1006, in a kingdom that had been under intermittent Viking attack for thirty years and was about to enter the worst of it. Sweyn Forkbeard and his son Canute were raiding the south coast every summer; the English crown was paying Danegeld in increasing amounts and the Vikings were taking more anyway.

The summer of 1011 they besieged Canterbury. Alphege was inside the city. The Vikings broke in by treachery after a three-week siege, sacked the cathedral, killed many of the monks, and took Alphege and several of his clergy hostage. They demanded three thousand pounds of silver in ransom for the archbishop — an enormous sum, which the diocese could only have raised by stripping every parish in Kent and starving every farmer south of the Thames. Alphege forbade his diocese to pay it. He sat in the Viking camp at Greenwich for seven months refusing to be ransomed.

On the night of April 19, 1012, the Viking host held a great drunken feast. The mead ran freely. They sent for Alphege. They demanded payment one last time. He refused one last time. The drunken Vikings began throwing the bones and skulls of the slaughtered cattle from the feast at him. They beat him with the flats of axes. At last a Viking named Thrum, who had been baptized the day before by Alphege himself in the Viking camp, took an axe and put it through the archbishop's skull, *for charity*, the chronicle says, *to end his sufferings*.

He was the first archbishop of Canterbury to die for the see. Thomas Becket, a hundred and fifty years later on the same hill, would call him to mind in his last sermon.

> *"I will not buy my life with the bread of the poor."* — Alphege, refusing the ransom
