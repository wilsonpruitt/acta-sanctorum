## March 2

# Chad of Mercia

*Bishop, c. 634–672*

He was a Northumbrian, the youngest of four brothers, all of whom became priests; the two best-known were Chad and his elder Cedd. They had been trained at Lindisfarne under Aidan, the Irish missionary bishop, and they took from Aidan the same lifelong habit: that a bishop walks, does not ride, and is poor.

Chad was for a few months bishop of York under disputed circumstances — the see had been awarded to him during the absence of Wilfrid, who returned to find his place taken. When Theodore of Tarsus arrived as archbishop of Canterbury and ruled the consecration irregular, Chad simply stepped down without protest. *I never thought myself worthy of it*, he said. *I undertook it only out of obedience.* Theodore was so moved by the answer that within a year he sent Chad north again, properly consecrated this time, as bishop of the Mercians.

He governed Mercia from a small wooden cell at Lichfield. He walked. Theodore eventually ordered him to ride — the diocese was vast and the bishop was no longer young — and Chad would only ride at the archbishop's express command, dismounting whenever he reached a settled place.

He died after three years. Bede preserves what he said on his deathbed. He had heard, he told the brothers, the music of his welcome; his elder brother Cedd was there waiting for him. *I am ready to go*, he said. *Let the singing be cheerful.*
