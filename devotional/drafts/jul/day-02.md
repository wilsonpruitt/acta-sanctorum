## July 2

# Otto of Bamberg

*Apostle of Pomerania, c. 1060–1139*

He was born in Swabia of a minor noble family, educated at a cathedral school of the upper Rhine, and went to Poland in his twenties as chaplain in the household of the Duchess Judith of Swabia. He spent a decade at Krakow, learned Slavic well enough to preach in it, and built such a reputation for administrative competence and pastoral gentleness that Henry IV of Germany asked for him as chancellor. He came to the imperial court in 1101, served through the worst years of the Investiture Controversy, refused to side openly with either his emperor or his pope, and was nominated to the see of Bamberg in 1102. He was reluctant — he held that the king should not nominate bishops — and accepted only after going to Rome on foot, submitting himself to Pope Paschal II, and receiving his pallium directly from the apostolic see.

He governed Bamberg thirty-six years. He rebuilt the cathedral after a fire, founded twenty monasteries, and hosted Bernard of Clairvaux during the schismatic conflict of 1135. The work for which the AASS remembers him on July 2 was different. In 1124, when he was sixty-four, the Polish duke Bolesław III asked him to lead a mission to the still-pagan Pomeranians of the Baltic coast, whom an earlier Spanish missionary had failed to convert. Otto agreed.

He went twice, the second time in 1128. He went in episcopal vestments, with a train of seventy persons, carrying gold plate and rich vestments — having concluded, from the failure of his predecessor, that the Pomeranians would not respect a missionary who arrived as a beggar. The strategy worked. He baptized perhaps twenty thousand persons in two summers, founded eleven churches between the Oder and the Persante, and trained a body of native Pomeranian-speaking priests before he left. He returned to Bamberg and governed eleven more years.

He died at Bamberg, June 30, 1139, in his eightieth year, and was buried in the cathedral he had rebuilt. The AASS day moves him to July 2 to keep clear of the Visitation in the late medieval calendar. The Pomeranian church he founded was destroyed by the Reformation. The Polish coast still calls him *Apostol Pomorza* — the apostle of Pomerania.

> *"It is not the bare foot but the present heart that converts a pagan."* — Otto, on his mission strategy
