## July 19

# Macrina the Younger

*Ascetic, sister of Basil and Gregory, c. 327–379*

She was the eldest of the ten children of Basil the Elder and Emmelia of Caesarea — a Cappadocian Christian household of the second rank of the senatorial aristocracy, with estates at Annisa in Pontus and connections through both grandmothers to the confessors of the Diocletianic persecution. She was named Macrina after her own grandmother, who had been a pupil of Gregory the Wonderworker and who had taught her the *Symbol* of the Cappadocian church before she could read. She was betrothed at twelve to a young man of the family circle who died before the marriage. She refused, at thirteen, to be betrothed again. She told her mother, the chronicler her brother Gregory of Nyssa says in the *Life of Macrina* he wrote about her, that the first betrothed was *not dead but present with the Lord*, and that a second engagement would be a kind of infidelity.

She kept the household. Her father died about 341. Her mother was left with ten children, four of them still small. Macrina, who was perhaps fourteen, took over the practical management of the estates while her mother retained the legal headship. She raised her own brothers and sisters: Basil (later the Great), Naucratius (a hunter and ascetic who died young), Peter (later bishop of Sebaste), Gregory (later bishop of Nyssa), and the others. She taught them the psalter and the New Testament before they were sent out to the city schools. When Basil came back from Athens at twenty-six full of his own brilliance, Gregory says, it was Macrina who took him down from his self-importance in a single afternoon and pointed him toward the monastic life.

She made the family estate at Annisa into a coenobitic community of about forty women — most of them former slaves and freedwomen of the household, freed by Macrina under a rule of common life. She herself worked at the same labors. She was, by Gregory's testimony, the chief theological mind of her own household, more decisive than Basil and more contemplative than Gregory; the dialogue Gregory wrote at her deathbed, *On the Soul and the Resurrection*, gives her speeches that he heard from her with his own ears.

She died at Annisa on July 19, 379, of a long wasting illness. Gregory had come from Nyssa to find her on a wooden plank in the small cell she had built for herself in the women's house. He buried her, the *Life* ends, in the grave her parents had been buried in, and her bones lie still in the Pontic earth.

> *"O Lord, thou hast loosed the dread of death from me, and made the end of this life the beginning of life."* — Macrina, dying, recorded by Gregory of Nyssa
