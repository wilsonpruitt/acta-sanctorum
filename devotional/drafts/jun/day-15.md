## June 15

# Landelinus of Crespin

*Merovingian robber-baron turned abbot, c. 625–685*

He was born of a Frankish landowning family in the country of Hainault, given as a boy to the school of Saint Aubert of Cambrai, and ran away from it in his teens. He fell in with a band of brigands in the forests between Cambrai and the Sambre, and for some years — the chronicler does not say how many, but no fewer than six, perhaps ten — he led them. He robbed travelers on the Roman roads. He robbed merchants of the Sambre wool trade. He raided villages. He had killed men, the *Vita* says plainly, though it does not number them; the number, by his own later confession, mattered less than that he could no longer count it.

A companion of the band died one night, and Landelinus — robbing then under the false name Maurosus — saw the man's soul carried off by demons. An angel stood over him in his sleep and told him to leave the band and return to Aubert as his spiritual father. Terrified by the vision, he left his companions and everything he had and went on foot to Cambrai, where he fell at the bishop's feet and begged penance. From there he walked to Rome on foot as the first work of that penance. He stopped at every shrine on the road and sometimes slept in the porches for days. He came back to Hainault a different man, was ordained at Cambrai by Aubert's successor Vindician, and was given title to a small clearing in the wood at Lobbes on the Sambre.

He built a monastery there with his own hands in 654, then a second at Aulne in 657, then a third at Wallers, then a fourth — the great house — at Crespin in 670, on land donated by a converted noble. He governed Crespin for fifteen years. He required of his monks only this: that they live by the work of their hands and refuse no traveler at the gate. He had been a robber on these roads; he made his houses into the first thing a stranger met on them. The men of his old band, the *Vita* says, came to him one by one over the years, several to die as monks under his rule. He died at Crespin, June 15, 685, having outlived his crimes by forty years and lost count, again, of the days he had spent in penance.

> *"Let not my absence sadden you, best brothers; the eternal Pastor, the Lord Jesus Christ, will be present to you wholly, if you strive to hold to his charity the more attentively."* — Landelinus to his monks
