## February 29

# Oswald of Worcester

*Bishop, Benedictine reformer, c. 925–992*

He was an Anglo-Dane, born of a Danish father who had settled in England in the generation after Alfred's wars and an English mother of noble blood. His uncle Oda was archbishop of Canterbury and recognized in the boy what the boy did not yet see in himself; he sent Oswald, when he was old enough, to the great Benedictine reform house of Fleury on the Loire to learn the new continental observance. Oswald came back ten years later with the Fleury Rule in his head and a slight French accent he never lost.

He was made bishop of Worcester in 961 and, eleven years later, archbishop of York as well, holding both sees with papal permission because the English Church did not yet have enough reformed bishops to spare. Together with Dunstan of Canterbury and Ethelwold of Winchester, Oswald drove the monastic reform that remade tenth-century English Christianity — replacing the lax secular canons of the cathedral chapters with Benedictine monks, founding new abbeys, copying manuscripts, restoring the daily office in houses where it had not been kept for fifty years. He founded or rebuilt Westbury, Ramsey, Pershore, Worcester, Winchcombe. He served as bishop for thirty-one years.

He kept a private practice that the people of Worcester knew about. Every week — Maundy Thursday or no — he would have twelve of the city's poor men brought to the cathedral cloister, and he would wash their feet on his knees. He kept it up to extreme old age, when his hands could barely hold the basin. He died doing it. On the morning of February 29, 992, he had washed the feet of eleven men. He bent to begin the twelfth and did not rise. The brothers who carried him to his bed said he was already gone.

> *"He died at the work he had always done."* — Byrhtferth, *Life of Oswald*
