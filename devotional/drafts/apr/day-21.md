## April 21

# Anselm of Canterbury

*Archbishop, doctor, 1033–1109*

He was born at Aosta in the Italian Alps, a sensitive boy whose mother died when he was twelve and whose father, on a quarrel, refused to let him enter the monastery. He ran away from home at twenty-three with one servant, crossed the Alps on foot, and presented himself eventually at the Norman abbey of Bec, where Lanfranc — who would later be his archbishop — was master of the school. Anselm took the habit. He never went back to Aosta.

He became prior of Bec at thirty, abbot at forty-five, archbishop of Canterbury at sixty — every promotion against his explicit and stated preference. He told the brothers, when they elected him to Bec, that they had chosen a man who would not do the work well; he told the bishops of England, when they elected him to Canterbury, that they had chosen one who could not.

What he did between offices was think. The *Monologion*, his first major work, set out to prove what could be known of God from reason alone. The *Proslogion*, written shortly after, gave the world the famous ontological argument: *that than which nothing greater can be thought* must exist, because if it did not, something greater could be thought. The *Cur Deus Homo* asked why God became man and answered, in a sustained argument that still shapes Western theology, that the satisfaction of divine justice required it. He invented an entire genre of Christian philosophy: *fides quaerens intellectum*, faith seeking understanding. He insisted he was not arguing himself toward faith but praying himself toward understanding what he already believed.

Twice exiled by English kings during the investiture controversy, he returned twice and finished his work. He died at Canterbury at seventy-six, Holy Wednesday, having that morning been read the gospel for the day.

> *"I do not seek to understand in order to believe; I believe in order to understand."* — Anselm, *Proslogion* I
