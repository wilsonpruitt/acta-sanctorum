## February 15

# Sigfrid of Sweden

*Missionary bishop, d. c. 1045*

He was an English priest — most likely from York, possibly from Glastonbury — when in the 1020s the English king's missionary office asked him to take a small band of English clergy north to Sweden. The Swedish king Olof Skötkonung had been baptized a generation earlier but the country was still effectively pagan; the mission would be slow and dangerous. Sigfrid took three of his nephews with him — Unaman, Sunaman, and Vinaman — and a few other priests. They sailed up the coast and crossed into Värend, in southern Sweden, where the local lords had asked for missionaries.

The work went well. Sigfrid baptized the regional king and built a small wooden church at Växjö, the first in the region. He left the three young priests to keep the church while he travelled further north into other Swedish provinces. He was a month away when a band of pagan men of Värend broke into the chapel at Växjö, killed the three young priests, took their heads, weighted them, and threw them into a nearby lake. Sigfrid came back to a community in mourning. He recovered the heads from the lake (the chronicle insists they were found together, the lake giving them up); he buried his nephews beside the chapel; he continued the mission.

King Olof, when he heard of the murders, ordered the perpetrators executed and their lands forfeit. Sigfrid went to him and asked for their lives. He could not, he said, baptize Sweden over the corpses of pagans whose hearts the gospel had not yet reached. The king relented on the executions but kept the fines. Sigfrid asked, then, that the fines be given to him to support the building of more churches. They were.

He died in old age, having outlived the murderers by decades, and was buried at Växjö beside his nephews.

> *"I cannot baptize Sweden over their corpses."* — Sigfrid, asking the king for the murderers' lives
