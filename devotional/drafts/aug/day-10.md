## August 10

# Lawrence of Rome

*Archdeacon and martyr, d. 258*

He was a Spaniard, the AASS Augusti II preserves the tradition — from Huesca in Aragon, born to Christian parents named Orentius and Patientia. He went to Rome young, was made acolyte and then deacon under Sixtus II, and at the time of the Valerian persecution of 258 served as the *archidiaconus* — the senior of the seven deacons of the Roman church, the man with charge of the church's plate, the church's archives, and the church's relief-rolls of the Roman poor.

Sixtus was arrested on August 6 in the catacomb of Praetextatus while keeping the Eucharist with his deacons. Lawrence, the AASS records from the contemporary Acts and the *Peristephanon* of Prudentius, ran after the soldiers as Sixtus was being led away and wept at his bishop's feet. *Whither goest thou, Father, without thy son? Whither, Priest, without thy deacon? Thou hast never offered the sacrifice without thy minister.* Sixtus answered him with the line the Roman office still keeps: *I am not leaving thee, son. After three days thou shalt follow.* Sixtus and the four deacons with him were beheaded that same day.

The Prefect of the city had Lawrence brought in within hours. The empire had decided to fund the Persian war by confiscating church property; the Prefect demanded the treasury of the Roman church. Lawrence asked for three days to gather it. He went out into the city and emptied the deacon's coffers among the poor of all twenty regions — the blind men of the *titulus Pudentis*, the lame of the *titulus Praxedis*, the widows of the Trastevere, the orphans of the Vatican fields, every name on the relief-roll he kept. On the third day he gathered them on the steps of the prefect's tribunal and presented them with the line that the *Acta* preserve as their core: *These are the treasures of the Church.*

He was tortured. The Prefect ordered him roasted on an iron gridiron over slow coals — a long death, designed to be visible. The tradition preserved by Ambrose, Augustine, Leo the Great, and Prudentius records that after some hours Lawrence raised his head and said, with the levity he had kept his whole life: *Assum est; versa et manduca* — *It is roasted on this side; turn me over and eat.* Augustine, who quotes the line in two separate sermons on Lawrence's feast, comments that it was not bravado but charity: the deacon was making it easier for his executioners to finish what they had to finish.

He died on the night of August 10, 258. They buried him on the Via Tiburtina. Constantine in the next century built the great basilica of San Lorenzo fuori le Mura over the spot, where his bones still lie.

> *"These are the treasures of the Church."* — Lawrence to the Prefect of Rome, the *Acta* preserved by Ambrose and Prudentius
