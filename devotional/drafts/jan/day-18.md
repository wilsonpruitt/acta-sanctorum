## January 18

# Deicolus of Lure

*Irish abbot in Burgundy, c. 530–625*

He was one of the twelve monks who left Bangor with Columbanus around the year 590 to bring monastic life into the Frankish kingdoms. Columbanus was about fifty then; Deicolus was somewhere between thirty and forty. For twenty years the band walked, preached, founded houses, and confronted kings. They settled longest at Luxeuil in the Vosges. When Columbanus rebuked the Burgundian king Theuderic II for keeping a series of concubines and refusing to take a wife, Theuderic's grandmother Brunhilda — who liked the arrangement — had the Irish driven out of the kingdom.

Deicolus could not keep up. He was old; the march to the coast was a hundred miles and more; he collapsed in the forest near Lure in eastern Burgundy. The story preserved by the locals is that he fell asleep against a tree and a wild boar lay down at his feet to warm him. A swineherd found him, took him in, and a wealthy Frankish lord named Werfhar gave him a small clearing in the forest. He built a hut. Other monks who had been scattered came back to him; he built a chapel; the chapel became a monastery; the monastery, Lutra — modern Lure — would last twelve hundred years.

The chroniclers remember him for his unfailing cheerfulness. Columbanus had asked him once, in the Vosges, why he was always smiling. Deicolus said: *because no one can take Christ from me*. It was the only formal sermon of his that has come down.

He lived to nearly a hundred. The Burgundian peasants who watched him grow old took the example, and stopped being surprised that an Irish exile abandoned in a forest had built them a house of God.
