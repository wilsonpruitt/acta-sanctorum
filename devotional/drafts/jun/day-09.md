## June 9

# Columba of Iona

*Irish abbot, apostle of the Picts, 521–597*

He was born of the Cenél Conaill in Donegal, great-great-grandson of Niall of the Nine Hostages, with a claim — had he wished to press it — on the high-kingship of Tara. He was baptized Crimthann and renamed in the cloister Colum Cille, *dove of the church*. He studied with Finnian of Moville, Gemman the bard, Finnian of Clonard, and Mobhi of Glasnevin: the whole roster of sixth-century Irish learning. He founded Derry, Durrow, perhaps Kells. His Irish verse is the earliest the language can give a date to.

He was forty-one when the trouble came. He had borrowed Finnian's psalter, copied it secretly through nights at the candle, and refused to return the copy. Diarmait, called to arbitrate, gave the famous judgment *to every cow her calf; to every book its copy* against him. Within months a kinsman of his, taking sanctuary at the king's court, was killed by Diarmait's men. Columba raised the Cenél Conaill against the high king and won the battle of Cúl Dreimne. Three thousand men died for the psalter and for the kinsman. His confessor laid a penance on him: leave Ireland, convert as many souls as had fallen in the battle, and never see the Irish coast again.

He left in 563 with twelve companions in a small *curragh* and made landfall on the island of Iona off the western coast of Pictish Scotland. From the monastery he founded there he sent missions inland for thirty-four years — to the Pictish high king Brude at Inverness, whom he converted in 564 after a contest of psalms and signs; to the Hebrides, to Argyll, to Northumbria — and trained the missionaries from whom Aidan of Lindisfarne would receive the Irish gospel that converted the English north.

He died at the altar of Iona at midnight of June 9, 597, having spent the day copying psalm 33. He had reached the verse *those who seek the Lord shall not be in want of any good thing*, laid down the pen, and added in the margin: *Let Baithene write the rest.* He went into the church for the night Office and fell at the altar steps. He had not seen Ireland in thirty-four years. The wind that night, the chronicler says, blew steadily from the west.
