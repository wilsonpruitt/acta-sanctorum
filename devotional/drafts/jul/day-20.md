## July 20

# Elijah the Prophet

*Tishbite of Gilead, c. 870 B.C.*

He came up out of the eastern hill country, out of Gilead beyond Jordan, with no genealogy and no priestly office, and stood unannounced before Ahab king of Israel in the third year of his reign and said, *As the Lord God of Israel liveth, before whom I stand, there shall be neither dew nor rain these years but according to my word.* The drought lasted three and a half years. He spent it hidden, first at the brook Cherith where ravens fed him, then with the widow at Zarephath whose handful of meal in the barrel and little oil in the cruse did not fail through the famine. He raised her son from death in the upper chamber. He went down to Mount Carmel in the third year and called the four hundred and fifty prophets of Baal to meet him there before all Israel.

He had two bullocks brought, gave them their choice, and let them prepare an altar. They cut their own flesh from morning to noon, calling on Baal in vain. He mocked them: *Cry aloud, for he is a god — peradventure he sleepeth, and must be awaked.* In the evening he rebuilt the broken altar of the Lord, dug a trench around it, drenched the sacrifice and the wood with twelve jars of water, and called: *Lord God of Abraham, Isaac, and of Israel, let it be known this day that thou art God, and that I am thy servant.* The fire of the Lord fell, and consumed the sacrifice, and the wood, and the stones, and the dust, and licked up the water that was in the trench. The people fell on their faces, and said, *The Lord, he is the God.*

He fled afterward into the wilderness, sat down under a juniper tree, asked to die. The angel fed him bread baked on coals and water in a cruse, and he walked forty days and forty nights on the strength of that meal to Horeb the mount of God. He stood in the cave there while a great wind broke the mountain — but the Lord was not in the wind; and after the wind an earthquake — but the Lord was not in the earthquake; and after the earthquake a fire — but the Lord was not in the fire; and after the fire *a still small voice*, and Elijah wrapped his face in his mantle, and the voice asked, *What doest thou here, Elijah?*

He passed his mantle to Elisha at the plowing. He went up by a whirlwind into heaven from the far side of Jordan with horses and a chariot of fire. The mantle fell. The Carmelites have him for their father. The fire on Carmel still falls, somewhere, on every soul that has stopped halting between two opinions.

> *"How long halt ye between two opinions? If the Lord be God, follow him."* — Elijah, *I Kings* 18:21
