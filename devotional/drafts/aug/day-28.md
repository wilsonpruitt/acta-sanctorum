## August 28

# Augustine of Hippo

*Bishop, doctor of grace, 354–430*

He was born on the 13th of November, 354, at the small Numidian town of Thagaste — modern Souk Ahras in eastern Algeria. His father Patricius was a pagan town councillor of modest means who would not convert until late in life; his mother Monica was a Christian whose persistence in prayer for husband and son the *Confessions* later recorded. He went away to school at Madaura, then to Carthage, where his father's friends scraped together enough to send him for the higher rhetorical training that was the only path open in late-Roman Africa for a boy of intellectual promise without family wealth.

He spent the next fifteen years in the wilderness Possidius and the *Confessions* describe in parallel. A common-law concubine he kept for thirteen years, who bore him his only son Adeodatus. The Manichaean dualist sect, in whose framework he tried for nine years to make sense of evil. The rhetorical career that took him from Thagaste back to Carthage, then to Rome, then in 384 to the imperial Milan court of Valentinian II, where as the official municipal rhetor he heard Ambrose preach for the first time. The slow, painful conversion through Plotinus, through Paul's letters, through the conversations with the African Pontitianus about the Egyptian monks — and at last the garden scene of August 386, when, weeping under a fig tree in his rented house, he heard a child's voice in the next house singing *tolle, lege* — take and read — and opened Paul's letter to the Romans at the chapter on the works of darkness. He was baptized by Ambrose at the Easter Vigil of 387.

He went home to Africa to found a small community of *servi Dei* on the family estate. His mother died at Ostia on the way home, after the conversation by the window the *Confessions* preserves as the one shared moment of mystical participation in his life. His son Adeodatus died at seventeen the following year. Augustine was thirty-six. He was kidnapped — almost literally — into the priesthood at Hippo in 391, when on a pastoral visit the assembled congregation surrounded him in the basilica and would not let him out until the elderly bishop Valerius had ordained him on the spot. Valerius made him co-bishop in 395, sole bishop a year later. He governed Hippo thirty-four years.

The works of his hand fill thirteen folio volumes. The *Confessions* in his early forties — the first sustained autobiographical interior narrative in the Latin tradition. The *De Trinitate* across twenty years of revision. The *City of God*, written across thirteen years in response to the sack of Rome in 410, to explain why the gospel does not promise temporal empires. The anti-Pelagian writings of the last fifteen years that set the foundation for every subsequent Western theology of grace. He died on the twenty-eighth of August, 430, in his episcopal house, with the Vandal army of Geiseric three months into the siege of Hippo. He had ordered the seven penitential psalms to be copied out in large letters and hung on the walls of his bedroom, so that he could read them on his deathbed.
