## June 11

# Barnabas the Apostle

*Cypriot Levite, companion of Paul, first century*

He appears first in the Acts of the Apostles, in chapter four, as Joseph, a Levite of Cyprus, who sold a field he owned and laid the price at the apostles' feet. The Twelve gave him another name. *Barnabas*, Luke explains, means *son of consolation* — *bar nehama*, in the Aramaic. He had a gift, and they had noticed it. When Saul of Tarsus arrived in Jerusalem some years later, freshly converted, and the disciples were afraid of him, it was Barnabas who took him by the hand and brought him in to the apostles, and explained what had happened on the road to Damascus. He stood, as the chronicler says, for the man no one else would yet stand for.

He was sent up to Antioch to investigate the rumor that Greeks were being baptized without circumcision. He arrived, saw, and approved. Then he walked to Tarsus, found Saul, and brought him back to Antioch with him to teach. It was there, under their joint year's teaching, that the disciples were first called Christians. From Antioch the church sent the two of them — Barnabas first, Paul second, in Luke's careful order — on the missionary journey through Cyprus and southern Asia Minor that opened the gentile mission of the church. They were taken for gods at Lystra after healing a man lame from birth, and only the speech Barnabas made — tearing his clothes, refusing the wreaths the priests of Zeus had brought — kept the crowd from sacrificing oxen to them.

He quarreled with Paul afterward over the boy John Mark, whom Paul refused to take on the second journey and Barnabas insisted on. They parted. Barnabas took Mark and sailed to his native Cyprus; Paul took Silas overland through Asia Minor; the world has had two streams of mission ever since, and neither was wrong. Mark would later write his Gospel at Rome, with Peter, who read it and approved it. The tradition, preserved in Tertullian and in the AASS, says Barnabas was stoned by Jews at Salamis around 61 and his body burned on a pyre — until even his bones, the Greek account says, were reduced to ash — yet found whole, buried by Mark with a copy of Matthew's Gospel laid on his breast. Four centuries later the body, the chronicler says, was found by another bishop of Salamis in exactly that condition, the Gospel intact, and Cyprus has held its bones since.

> *"He was a good man, full of the Holy Spirit and of faith."* — Luke, *Acts* 11:24
