## February 19

# Conrad of Piacenza

*Franciscan hermit, 1290–1351*

He was a Piacenzan nobleman, married young to a young woman named Euphrosyne, and he spent his early twenties hunting in the country estates north of the city. One autumn afternoon he and his companions were trying to flush game out of a thicket. They set fire to the brush. The wind rose. The fire spread to the surrounding fields, then to a village, then to a stretch of forest. By nightfall the chronicle is reckoning the cost in burned crops, lost houses, and one dead farmhand.

A peasant who had been seen near the original thicket was arrested. The magistrate sentenced him to death. The day before the execution, Conrad — who had been silent through the trial out of cowardice — went to the court and confessed that the fire was his. The peasant was released; Conrad was fined the value of his estates and a great deal more. He paid. He had nothing left.

He and Euphrosyne, after the ruin, made a decision. She entered the Poor Clares at Piacenza. Conrad became a Franciscan tertiary and walked south. He stopped at last on a hillside outside Noto in Sicily, in a cave that had once been a pagan shrine, and stayed there forty years. He prayed. He healed the sick who came to him. He counseled the bishop of Syracuse and several visiting pilgrims and the local shepherds in roughly equal proportion. The Sicilians, who appreciate this kind of saint, called him *il Beato Conrado* well before Rome confirmed it.

He died kneeling in his cave at sixty. The peasants of Noto buried him in the cave, walled it up, and kept the key.

He had set the fire that almost cost an innocent man his life. The rest of his own life he spent letting the same fire purify what it had failed to destroy.

> *"What I burned was my country; what was burned in me is who I was."* — Conrad, in the cave
