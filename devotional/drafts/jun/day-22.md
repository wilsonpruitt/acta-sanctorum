## June 22

# Paulinus of Nola

*Senator, poet, bishop of Campania, c. 354–431*

He was Meropius Pontius Paulinus, of a senatorial Gallo-Roman family with estates in Aquitaine, Italy, and Spain. He was rich beyond ordinary measure. He had been a pupil of the poet Ausonius at Bordeaux, made consul-suffect at Rome in his late twenties, married a Spanish noblewoman named Therasia, and held a string of provincial governorships. He could have lived out the imperial career arc and been forgotten by everyone but the prosopographers. He was forty when their only son died at eight days old. Husband and wife buried the child at Alcalá in Spain and walked, that summer, to the shrine of Saint Felix at Nola near Naples, where he had once as a young governor felt a pull he had not explained even to himself.

They came home and sold everything. He freed his slaves with pensions. He gave the cash to the poor and to the churches of his three provinces. He kept only one small plot of land at Nola itself, on which stood the shrine, and walked back to it on foot in 395 with Therasia. They built two small huts of clay and reeds in the orchard behind the shrine and went into them. They lived thirty-six more years. He was ordained, by force, in Spain on a Christmas Eve in 394; he was elected bishop of Nola, again with reluctance, about 410. Therasia died sometime after 415. He governed Nola through the Vandal raids on Italy, the fall of Rome in 410 (refugees from which he housed by the hundred in the abandoned senatorial villas of Campania), and the famine years that followed.

He wrote letters to everyone — to Augustine in Africa, to Jerome at Bethlehem, to Sulpicius Severus in Aquitaine, to Martin of Tours, to Ambrose at Milan — and his fifty-one surviving letters are among the most affectionate documents of the Latin patristic world. He wrote thirty-three poems for the annual feast of Saint Felix on January 14, one a year, almost without interruption, for thirty-three years. He sold himself once into Vandal slavery, the chronicler says, to ransom the only son of a widow of Nola whom the Vandals had taken; the Vandal chief, on discovering whom he had bought, sent him back. He died at Nola, June 22, 431, having outlived the imperial world he had been born into and built another one in its orchard.

> *"I have prepared a lamp for my Christ."* — Paulinus's last words, sung at the hour of the evening lamps (Uranius, *Life of Paulinus*)
