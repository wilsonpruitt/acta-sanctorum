## January 19

# Wulfstan of Worcester

*Bishop, Benedictine, 1008–1095*

He was the only Anglo-Saxon bishop who kept his see after the Norman Conquest, and he kept it by being so transparently saintly that Lanfranc gave up trying to depose him. The Normans had a procedure for old English bishops: gentle pressure, a language test, an accusation of insufficient learning, replacement. Wulfstan, when Lanfranc summoned him to surrender his pastoral staff at Edward the Confessor's tomb, set the staff down on the tomb and asked Edward to keep it for him. The story — most likely embellished, certainly believed — was that no one but Wulfstan could lift it again.

What was true beneath the legend is that no one else wanted his job once they had seen him at it. He preached in English when the Latin was lost on his hearers. He visited every parish in his diocese on horseback every year, sleeping in the parsonages and eating with the parish priests. He fasted hard, prayed long, slept little, and rarely sat down. With the help of his old friend Lanfranc — who came around — he led the campaign that ended the slave trade out of the port of Bristol, where English boys and girls had been sold to Ireland for two generations. He preached against it in Bristol itself, on the docks, for weeks, until the merchants relented.

He served as bishop of Worcester for thirty-two years. He rebuilt the cathedral. He buried his Anglo-Saxon people one by one and learned to bury his Norman ones too. He died in his eighties on January 19, 1095, having outlived nearly everyone who had ever known him as anything but the bishop.

> *"Why are you afraid? Christ is still on his throne."* — Wulfstan to a Norman noble, on hearing of a rebellion
