## June 27

# Ladislaus I of Hungary

*King and lawgiver of the Magyars, 1040–1095*

He was the second son of King Béla I of Hungary, born in exile at the Polish court at Krakow, where his father had taken refuge during the long disputed succession of the Árpád dynasty. He returned to Hungary as a boy when his father took the throne in 1060, and in the bitter dynastic wars that followed his father's death he served as duke under his elder brother Géza I against their cousin Solomon. The kingdom was unstable. The Magyars were two generations Christian and not yet entirely Christian. The Cumans and Pechenegs raided the eastern marches almost every year. Ladislaus took the crown on his brother's death in 1077, at thirty-seven, and held it eighteen years.

He was, the chronicler Hartvic says, six feet four inches tall, of immense physical strength, gentle of speech, and a man whom his soldiers obeyed because he was not afraid to be among them. He fought three campaigns against the Cumans and broke the back of their raiding power; the legend of Ladislaus tearing a captured Hungarian girl out of a Cuman warrior's saddle and killing the warrior with his bare hands at the battle of Kerlés in 1068 (which took place under his brother but in which he led the Hungarian wing) was painted on the walls of half the parish churches of medieval Hungary for the next four centuries.

He prepared a crusade in 1095, having been elected commander by an alliance of Polish, Bohemian, and German princes, but died on the road north of an illness, on the second-to-last day of June. His feast settled on June 27 because his death fell on the feast of Saints Peter and Paul (the 29th), whose vigil and octave crowded out the days around it, and because his body was raised from the earth, by sentence of the Apostolic See, on June 27 in 1192 — the year of his canonization. He had been the king his people remembered as *Szent László*, holy László, the one who had carried both the sword and the law for them at once.

> *"Fight with most valiant spirits, and seize victory under good auspices; but I betake myself to fight today for the good and safety of the kingdom, so that you shall behold me either a victorious duke, or beautifully falling among the thickest of the enemy."* — Ladislaus to his soldiers before battle
