## June 12

# Leo III, Pope

*Roman pontiff, crowner of Charlemagne, c. 750–816*

He was a Roman of modest birth — the son of a man named Azuppius — who rose through the Lateran chancery on competence rather than family. He had served thirty years in the curia, as treasurer and then cardinal-priest of Santa Susanna, when on the death of Hadrian I in December 795 he was elected pope by acclamation and consecrated the next day, the swiftest succession in centuries. He sent the keys of the confession of Saint Peter and the standard of the city of Rome to Charlemagne, king of the Franks, as a sign that the Roman pontiff acknowledged the Frankish king as protector of the Holy See. Charlemagne returned a courteous letter and a large gift of plate from the Avar treasure. The two never met.

Four years later the Roman nobility — relatives of his predecessor, who had been frozen out of the chancery — ambushed Leo during a procession on the feast of Saint Mark, 799. They dragged him from his horse, tried to cut out his tongue and gouge out his eyes, left him for dead in the street. Romans of the city rescued him in the dark and hid him in the monastery of Saint Erasmus on the Caelian. He recovered, miraculously, the chronicler says, with sight and speech intact, and slipped north over the Alps with a small Frankish escort to the king's court at Paderborn. Charlemagne received him with extraordinary honor and sent him home with an escort and an investigation commission. Leo was cleared by an oath of self-purgation at Saint Peter's in December 800. Two days later, on Christmas, as he rose from the *Te Deum* of the third Mass of the feast, he set the imperial crown of the West upon Charlemagne's head and acclaimed him *Augustus*.

He governed twenty years. He defended the *Filioque* clause in the creed against Greek protest but refused to insert it in the Roman liturgy in his lifetime, on the principle that the council of Nicaea had spoken. He sheltered Anglo-Saxon refugees from the Viking raids on Northumbria. He rebuilt the Lateran *triclinium* with the famous apse mosaic that survives in fragment today — Christ commissioning the apostles, Saint Peter handing the *pallium* to Leo and the standard to Charlemagne — a programmatic image of the bond he had made on Christmas Day. He died at the Lateran, June 12, 816.

> *"To Charles the most Pious, Augustus, crowned by God, the great and peaceful Emperor, life and victory."* — the acclamation of the Roman people at Charlemagne's coronation, Christmas 800 (Anastasius the Librarian)
