## January 4

# Pharaildis of Ghent

*Virgin, widow, royal Frankish, c. 650–740*

She was born to King Theuderic of Lotharingia and his queen Amelberga, raised from the font by her kinswoman Gertrude, and instructed in letters at a court that expected princesses to marry where the king pointed. Pharaildis had pointed elsewhere. From her mother's arms, the chronicler insists, she had given herself entire to Christ. When her father pressed her toward a marriage of his own arranging — to a nobleman named Guido — she could not refuse him in the open, but on the wedding night she knelt and prayed.

The prayer she made, the biographer preserves in four lines of awkward verse: that the same God who made the fabric of the age would grant his handmaid to keep her promise. The grace held. For thirty years she continued to share Guido's house, slipping out at midnight or cockcrow to the monastery for vigils and returning before dawn. He thought it an affair, and beat her for years. She did not leave; she did not strike back; she asked God to put an end to him. Not long after, riding hard to a hunt, he was thrown from his horse, broke himself badly, and died.

Pharaildis took the widow's veil and lived almost ninety years. The most-remembered story from her widowhood is small. In a winter field she gathered some wild geese with her staff as if they were sheep, drove them home, and forbade her servants to harm them. One she found missing — its bones and feathers in a cooking-pot — and she put them back together and sent it on its way. Her sisters Gudula and Reineldis and her brother Emebertus would all become saints in the same Belgian church.

She is painted, where her cult survived the Calvinist destruction of her shrine at Ghent, with a goose at her feet. The small miracle is what the peasants remembered. The long endurance, less easy to paint, was what carried her there.

> *"Supreme Father, King, Light, and Source of days, by whose command the fabric of this age stands: grant to your grateful handmaid to enjoy virginity."* — Pharaildis, on her wedding night
