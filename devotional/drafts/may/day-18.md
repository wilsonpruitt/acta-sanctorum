## May 18

# John I, Pope

*Roman pontiff and martyr, d. 526*

He was an old Tuscan deacon when the clergy of Rome elected him to the chair of Peter in August 523, succeeding Hormisdas at a moment when the Italian peninsula was held by an Arian king and the imperial capital had moved seven hundred miles east. Theodoric the Ostrogoth had governed Italy for thirty years with a tolerable mildness, but at the end of his reign the news from Constantinople — that the emperor Justin had begun to harass the Arians of the East — made him suddenly afraid for his own. He summoned the new pope to Ravenna and ordered him to lead a Roman embassy to the emperor: not on behalf of orthodoxy, but on behalf of the Arian heretics whom Theodoric ruled.

John went. He was the first bishop of Rome to set foot in Constantinople. He was received by Justin with extraordinary honors — the emperor prostrated himself before him in the city square — and he crowned Justin in the basilica of Hagia Sophia on Easter Day, 526, the first imperial coronation by a pope. He celebrated the Roman rite at the altar, taking precedence over the patriarch. What he did not do was the thing Theodoric had sent him to do. He returned to Italy having quietly failed the king's commission, having declined to ask the emperor to restore the seized basilicas to Arian use.

Theodoric had him met at the dock at Ravenna, stripped of his retinue, and shut in a cell. John was old and the journey had been long. He died there within weeks, on May 18, of hunger and exhaustion, never having returned to Rome. The Romans brought his body home and buried him in the portico of Saint Peter's. The Acta number him among the martyrs, though no sword fell. He had been killed by a refusal — the refusal to use the office of Peter to enlarge the reach of a heresy, even when the price of refusing was a dungeon.

> *"What you are about to do, King, do quickly; behold, I stand in your sight. This I do not promise you that I will do, nor will I tell him."* — Pope John I, to Theodoric
