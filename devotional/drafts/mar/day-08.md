## March 8

# Felix of Dunwich

*Apostle of East Anglia, d. 647*

He was a Burgundian by birth, a priest already when he met Sigebert of East Anglia in Gaul. Sigebert was at that moment a king with no kingdom — exiled by his half-brother, baptized in his exile by Frankish bishops, waiting for the call home. The call came in 631. Sigebert recovered his throne, and Felix went with him.

Archbishop Honorius of Canterbury formally consecrated Felix bishop and sent him to the East Anglian kingdom, where Christianity had reached the court only and not yet the country. Felix set up his see at Dunwich, on the Suffolk coast — then a sizeable trading port, long since lost to the North Sea — and built churches. He travelled. He founded a school on the model of the Frankish episcopal schools he had grown up in, the first English school to teach Latin grammar, and Bede records that the kingdom was almost entirely Christian within the seventeen years of Felix's episcopate. Sigebert himself eventually resigned the crown to become a monk at the monastery Felix had helped him establish at Beodricsworth — modern Bury St Edmunds.

Bede plays with the name. *Felix*, he writes — *as his name signified, he brought happiness to that province.* East Anglia was Christian and literate when Felix died in 647; both gifts came in through the same door.

> *"His name signified what he was: he brought happiness to that province."* — Bede, *Ecclesiastical History* II.15
