## October 27

# Frumentius of Ethiopia

*Apostle of the Aksumites, first bishop of Ethiopia, c. 305–c. 380*

He was a young Christian Tyrian boy travelling on a Red Sea trading-ship with his uncle Meropius, an Alexandrian philosopher hired to map the unsurveyed coastlines of the African and South Arabian shores for the imperial geographers. The boy was perhaps twelve years old; his older brother Edesius, perhaps fourteen, was also on board. Sometime around 316 the ship put in for water at a small port on the African coast of the Red Sea. The local tribe, hearing that the Romans had broken their treaty with the Aksumite kingdom, attacked the ship. The crew was killed. The two boys, found sitting under a tree on the shore reading their books, were spared and brought up to the king at Aksum.

The king of Aksum was Ella Amida. He took to the boys. He kept Edesius as his cup-bearer and Frumentius as his secretary and treasurer. They lived in the palace for the next twenty years, learning Ge'ez, the court administration, the trade routes south to the Nile and east to Yemen. When Ella Amida died around 330, leaving an infant son under the regency of the queen mother Sophia, the queen begged the two foreigners to stay on until the boy Ezana came of age. They agreed. For the next decade Frumentius effectively ran the kingdom.

He used the office to plant the gospel. The Roman traders coming through Aksum had been Christian for two generations; Frumentius gave them land for chapels, exempted them from taxes for liturgical purposes, encouraged them to gather Sunday assemblies in the towns where they did business. By the time Ezana came of age around 340 there was a substantial Christian community in Aksum and along the coast at Adulis. The two brothers asked permission to go home. Edesius went back to Tyre; Frumentius went down to Alexandria.

He went to see the new patriarch — Athanasius, just elected at thirty-three — and laid the situation in front of him. He did not ask for himself; he asked for a bishop. Athanasius listened and answered, in the line Rufinus preserves: *And what other man shall we find as fit, in whom is the Spirit of God as in you, who can finish what you have begun?* He consecrated Frumentius bishop of Aksum on the spot. Frumentius spent the next forty years building the Ethiopian church, baptizing King Ezana in the late 340s, training the first generation of Ethiopian-born clergy, translating the gospels into Ge'ez. Ezana set the cross on his coinage. The Ethiopian Orthodox Church, the second-oldest national Christian community after Armenia, traces its episcopate to him. They call him *Abuna Salama,* the Father of Peace.
