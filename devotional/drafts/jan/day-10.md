## January 10

# William of Bourges

*Archbishop, Cistercian, c. 1155–1209*

He was a younger son of the noble house of Nevers, given an honorary canonry while still a boy and a real one in his early twenties; he might have lived out the unremarkable life of a regular canon at Soissons and never been heard of. The death of his elder brother brought the inheritance toward him; he refused it. He left Soissons, walked across France, and presented himself at the gates of the Cistercian abbey of Pontigny. Twenty years later, when the see of Bourges fell vacant, he was abbot of Châalis. The chapter of Bourges elected him archbishop. He went into hiding. The pope wrote ordering him to accept. He accepted.

He kept the Cistercian habit under his pallium and ate at the monastic table when he could. He preached against the Albigensian errors spreading south of him without rancor, by patient instruction; he was reluctant to bless the armed crusade that Innocent III was beginning to prepare against them, preferring conversion to conquest. He arbitrated quarrels between Philip II of France and the pope, and was respected by both for the visible absence of his own interest in the outcome.

He died nine years after his consecration, worn out, on the night of January 10. The chronicle says he had spent the day hearing confessions and refused to break his fast until vespers. The community at Bourges buried him in the cathedral; pilgrims began coming almost at once, and Honorius III canonized him within a decade — a speed of recognition that had no parallel in his century.

What William offered his diocese was the witness of an office held lightly, in the constant awareness that it was on loan.

> *"Lord, you have given me this charge against my will; let me at least bear it as you would have me bear it."* — William, after his consecration
