## September 1

# Arcanus and Aegidius of Borgo San Sepolcro

*Hermit-pilgrims, founders of an Umbrian town, c. 936–c. 960*

They met, the chronicle says, at the Holy Sepulchre. Arcanus was a Greek from Arcadia; Aegidius was a Spaniard. Both had come to Jerusalem on pilgrimage, both stayed to pray at the empty tomb, both walked out one morning carrying a satchel of relics they had been given and the conviction that they were not finished travelling. They went to Rome together. They venerated the apostles' tombs. They turned north, intending to go on to Venice and from there to take the longer way home.

In Umbria, between Arezzo and the Tiber, they stepped off the road into a wood to rest at a clear spring. Arcanus fell asleep. He woke believing he had heard a voice — *this place is holy; stop here; build an altar.* He looked at the satchel of relics he had hung on a low branch. As he watched (so the *Annals of Borgo* tell it) the satchel slipped its cord and rose into the air. It would not come down until the two of them, half terrified, half laughing, vowed they would build a church on the spot. Then it descended, and they began to gather stones.

The Bollandists handle the satchel story with the polite skepticism it deserves — Leo X, when he made the place a bishopric in 1515, mentioned "a divine vision" but spoke of the flying relics not at all. What no one disputes is what came afterwards. The chapel rose; pilgrims found it; cottages went up around it; a town grew. They named it for the empty tomb the two pilgrims had left behind in Jerusalem: *Borgo del Santo Sepolcro,* the burgh of the Holy Sepulchre. Camaldulese monks took over the chapel a generation later. The town is still there. It still bears the name.

The two pilgrims kept walking until they didn't. *This place is holy; stop here.* Most lives are made by knowing when to stop.

> *"Locus hic sanctus est, hic siste gradum, et aram erige."* — *the voice Arcanus heard at the spring*
