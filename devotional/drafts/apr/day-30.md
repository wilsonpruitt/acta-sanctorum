## April 30

# Pius V, Pope

*Dominican, Counter-Reformer, 1504–1572*

He was Antonio Ghislieri, born to a poor family at Bosco Marengo in Piedmont, and his early childhood was spent tending sheep on the family's small plot. He met the Dominicans by accident at fourteen when one of them paid for the boy to be given a real education, and the boy entered the order in gratitude. He proved a remarkable scholar. He taught philosophy and theology in Dominican houses for sixteen years. He became, in his forties, a papal inquisitor in northern Italy; he was scrupulous in his judgments, harsh on his own conduct, fasting and walking everywhere himself when his colleagues took carriages. Paul IV made him cardinal in 1557. Pius IV made him Grand Inquisitor of the Roman Inquisition in 1558.

He was elected pope in 1566. He was sixty-two. He wore the white wool Dominican habit under his papal robes for the rest of his life — he had been a Dominican longer than he had been a churchman — and he ran the papal household as if it were a convent. He cut court expenses. He gave the savings to the poor of Rome. He insisted that the bishops actually reside in their dioceses, that the seminaries the Council of Trent had decreed actually open, that the catechism actually be printed and used. He gave the Latin Church the Roman Missal of 1570 — the *Missal of Pius V*, the form that would govern Catholic worship for the next four centuries.

In 1571 he formed the Holy League against the Ottoman fleet that had taken Cyprus and was preparing to take more. The combined Christian fleet under Don Juan of Austria defeated the Ottomans at Lepanto on the seventh of October. Pius, in Rome, knew the outcome before the news arrived — he interrupted a meeting that afternoon, walked to the window, said *let us thank God; we have won a great victory*, and would not say more.

He died six months later, sixty-eight, asking only for the prayers of his old order.

> *"I am a Dominican; let me die in this habit."* — Pius V, before his death
