## May 7

# Benedict II

*Pope, d. 686*

He had soldiered for the Church, the biographer says, from his earliest years — a Roman boy given to the Scriptures and the chant, raised through the priesthood by nothing but his merit, until the city that had watched him grow up made him its bishop and the eighty-third pope. He was a lover of poverty, gentle and patient, with what Anastasius calls "a hand most large": whatever came to him went out again to the poor. He restored the crumbling basilicas of Peter and of Laurence, and made two gold chalices for the altar, each a pound in weight, for a Church he served as steward and never as owner.

His one great act was to give the papacy back a measure of its own freedom. For generations a pope elected at Rome had to wait — sometimes for months, across the whole breadth of the empire — for the emperor at Constantinople to confirm him before he could be ordained. Benedict obtained from the emperor Constantine the concession that whoever the Church chose should be ordained at once, without the long delay. He had been pope barely ten months when he died, on the twenty-fifth of March, 686.

The signs his biographer remembered from those months were all of brevity and warning: a star standing by the Pleiades in the night, the winter sky darkening between Christmas and Epiphany as though the moon had passed under a cloud, and Vesuvius in Campania throwing up ash by day that buried the country around it. His own epitaph caught the same note — a man who gleamed like a lightning-flash, who had begun more than the short time allowed him could finish. They moved his body the next May to Saint Peter's, where it lies still.

> *In the likeness of a lightning-flash you gleam with the mind's splendour; but the greater things, begun in a small time, flow away.* — from his epitaph
