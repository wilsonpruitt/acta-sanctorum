## July 23

# Bridget of Sweden

*Visionary and founder, 1303–1373*

She was Birgitta Birgersdotter, of the great Swedish noble house of Folkunga, daughter of a *lagman* of Uppland who governed his province with the law-codes the Folkungas had drafted. She was given in marriage at thirteen to the noble Ulf Gudmarsson and bore him eight children over twenty-two years. Two of those children — Karl and Catherine — would become important saints in their own right. The marriage was, by every account, deeply affectionate. She had begun receiving visions of Christ on the cross from the age of seven; she told only her confessor; she ran the great Folkunga estate, governed her household, raised her children, and kept the visions to herself for forty years.

She was lady-in-waiting at the Swedish court under King Magnus Eriksson and his wife Blanche of Namur for several years in her thirties. The court was, by the *Vita* her confessor wrote, careless; she withdrew when she could. Her husband Ulf was struck down by a fever in 1344 returning from a pilgrimage they had made together to Compostella, and she was widowed at forty-one. She put on the Cistercian habit of a tertiary, gave away her wardrobe and her jewels, retired to a small house at Alvastra near the lake of Vättern, and for two years sat in the chapel of the Cistercian abbey there transcribing the visions that had now begun to come almost daily.

The Christ of her visions told her to found a new order — for women primarily, with a small attached community of priests for the women's spiritual care — and to take the rule to Rome. She walked to Rome in 1349 by way of Cologne and the Alps and never returned. She lived in Rome for the remaining twenty-four years of her life, in a small house near the Campo dei Fiori that still stands. She wrote, through her two Swedish chaplains who took the dictation in Latin from her Swedish, the eight books of the *Revelations* — visions of Christ's nativity, his passion, his bride the church, the popes of her century at Avignon (whom she scolded for not returning to Rome), the kings of Europe (whom she scolded for the war between England and France), and the angels and saints of her own household. She founded the *Order of the Most Holy Savior* — the *Bridgettines* — and saw, before she died, her first daughter Catherine professed in it.

She died at her house in Rome, July 23, 1373, at seventy. Her daughter Catherine carried her body home to Sweden by sea and overland; the journey took a year. They buried her at Vadstena where the mother-house of the order had been chartered. The order is still there.

> *"My daughter, the world is not deceived by the great; it is deceived by the small that the great refuse to see."* — Bridget, *Revelations* IV.49
